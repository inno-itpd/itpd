# Assignment 4 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignment 4 and the requirements it depends on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignment and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A4-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the week's scope to the links that carry the trace.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
An entry that reverses an `A3-DEC` records the whole reversal, so `A4-DEC-027` restates the clauses of `A3-DEC-014` that stay standing, because a reversal cannot be partial.
Mechanical edits — routing, backlog housekeeping, and the dates a decision here sets — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A4-DEC-001: Week 4 Is The Requirements, The Architecture, And The Fourth Release

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 4 covers the functional requirements, the quality requirements, the architectural decision records, the architecture document's drivers and ADR index, the glossary, the sprint ceremonies, the team's `AGENTS.md`, `REL-04`, and the deadline change; the quality requirement tests, their testing plan, the container, component, and sequence views, and continuous integration (`actionlint` and the product-code CI) are Assignment 5's.
- **Why:** the week's lecture is software quality, so the week applies the quality model, the six-part scenario, the drivers, and the architectural decision records rather than describing them.
  The tests, the testing plan, and the views rest on the quality requirements and arrive with the verification lecture in Week 5, so they follow the week that writes those requirements.
  The ceremonies, the estimates, the glossary, and the team's `AGENTS.md` are the week's own process and repository records, which is why the week ends in `REL-04` with its planning, its retrospective, and its deadline rather than in a set of documents with no sprint around them.
- **Alternatives considered:** putting the quality requirement tests and the testing plan in Week 4 beside the requirements, rejected because the week would carry the verification work the next lecture teaches and the testing plan's status would be written before anything could fill it; and leaving the ceremonies and the team's `AGENTS.md` to Week 5, rejected because the sprint's planning and velocity would then have no record and the repository's agent instructions would arrive after the team has worked a week without them.
- **Where it lands:** [`assignments/assignment-4.md`](../../assignments/assignment-4.md), with the week's deliverables in [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-4-oct-16--oct-22-software-quality-architecture--next-product-release), and the deferred scope in [`a5-plan.md`](../plans/a5-plan.md).

## A4-DEC-002: Seven Flat Requirements Files Own The New Rules

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the new rules live in seven flat files under `requirements/` — `architecture-requirements.md`, `functional-requirements-requirements.md`, `quality-requirements-requirements.md`, `adr-requirements.md`, `sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md`, `agent-instructions-requirements.md`, and `glossary-requirements.md` — each the single owner of its group, with `**Since: W4**` markers, a table of contents, `Required` / `Recommended` / `Example` labels, and one full example at its end.
- **Why:** the seven groups have their own lifecycles, fields, and citation rules, so one file could not own them without giving some rule a second owner.
  Every requirements file is flat beside the others, so a student who has found one has found them all, and every router names the group in one row.
  An example at the end of each file is what makes the group's record readable without assembling it from the rules.
- **Alternatives considered:** extending an existing requirements file with the new groups, rejected because each group's citation links differ from the file that would host it, as [`A3-DEC-017`](a3.md#a3-dec-017-the-new-requirements-files-each-own-one-artifact-group) found for the Week 3 files; and nesting the new files in directories by artifact, rejected because every requirements file is flat and the routers would have to learn a second shape.
- **Where it lands:** [Architecture Requirements](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md), [Functional Requirements](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md), [Quality Requirements](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md), [ADR Requirements](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md), [Sprint Ceremonies Requirements](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md), [Agent Instructions Requirements](../../requirements/agent-instructions-requirements.md), and [Glossary Requirements](../../requirements/glossary-requirements.md), with the routing rule in [the README](../../README.md#the-rules), [course/rules.md](../rules.md#where-the-rules-live), and [SKILL.md](../../SKILL.md#where-each-kind-of-work-is-ruled).

## A4-DEC-003: Functional Requirements Exist, Reversing A3-DEC-014

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Reverses:** [A3-DEC-014](a3.md#a3-dec-014-the-stack-is-a-decision-and-there-is-no-functional-requirements-file), in full.
- **Decision:** `FR-nnn` exists as a family of its own: an FR is a file under `docs/functional-requirements/fr/` that states behavior independently of the story that needed it, and this entry reverses `A3-DEC-014` in full, so its clause that stories with their acceptance criteria are the functional requirements stops holding.
- **Why:** the architecture has to account for requirements, and a story is the customer's test rather than the specification that a record can answer, so the requirements the design is measured against need their own family.
  An FR outlives the story that produced it, which is why it states behavior a reader can check without opening the issue.
  A reversal cannot be partial, so this entry takes the whole of `A3-DEC-014` and `A4-DEC-027` restates the clauses that stay standing.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the stories as the only requirements and letting an ADR's `**Addresses:**` list stories, rejected because a story closes once the customer has accepted it while the behavior it asked for stays in the product; and a single `docs/functional-requirements.md` collected from stories and constraints, the option `A3-DEC-014` deferred, rejected because one file cannot carry a record's own fields, criteria, and anchors.
- **Where it lands:** [Where Functional Requirements Live](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#where-functional-requirements-live), [The Functional Requirement](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#the-functional-requirement), and [Part 4](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-4-write-the-functional-requirements).

## A4-DEC-004: A Functional Requirement Is Independent Of The Story That Needed It

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** an FR specifies behavior without the story that produced it: it has no story field, restates no story, may cite the origin story in `**Traces to:**` beside the `DEC-nnn`, `CON-nn`, or meeting report that produced it and the `ADR-nnn` that constrains it, names the containers it runs in, and carries its own `### AC-nn` criteria with anchors.
- **Why:** the FR is what the architecture answers, so it has to say what the system does rather than what a customer asked for in one week.
  The story side owns `### Specified by`, so one fact lives on one side and the two cannot drift.
  `**Containers:**` is what lets a reader see which deployable unit carries the requirement, per behavior, while the container view lists the units and the responsibilities rather than the behavior, and a criterion with an anchor can be cited by a task or a pull request, which a criterion inside a closed story cannot.
- **Alternatives considered:** giving the FR a `**Stories:**` field, rejected because the story already names the FRs that specify it and the second list would go stale; and letting the FR repeat its story's criteria, rejected because the two would then be maintained twice for different readers.
- **Where it lands:** [The Functional Requirement](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#the-functional-requirement), [Behavior](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#behavior), and [Acceptance Criteria](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#acceptance-criteria).

## A4-DEC-005: A Quality Requirement Is One ISO Pair And One Scenario

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a quality requirement is one file at `docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-nn.md`, headed with the outcome in one sentence, carrying exactly one ISO/IEC 25010:2023 characteristic and sub-characteristic pair from the allowed table, both priorities, and the six-part quality attribute scenario under `## Scenario`; `docs/quality-requirements/README.md` is the index and the priority matrix.
- **Why:** the lecture teaches the ISO/IEC 25010 model and the six-part scenario, so the record has to hold both if a reviewer is to fail it against a measure that runs.
  One pair per requirement keeps each record's meaning single, and the allowed table keeps the course's Title Case labels and the standard's own terms in step.
  The index being the matrix means the priorities are read where the records are listed, rather than in a second table that could disagree.
- **Alternatives considered:** one quality requirements document holding every record, rejected because a requirement's status, priorities, and scenario belong to its own file and a link has to reach it; and a free-text characteristic field, rejected because the pairs are the standard's and a made-up label cannot be checked against it.
- **Where it lands:** [Where Quality Requirements Live](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#where-quality-requirements-live), [The Quality Requirement](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirement), [The Quality Attribute Scenario](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-attribute-scenario), and [The Allowed Pairs](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-allowed-pairs).

## A4-DEC-006: An Architectural Decision Record Lists The ASRs It Answers

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** an ADR is one file at `docs/architecture/adr/ADR-nnn.md` in the MADR shape, with `**Status:**`, `**Superseded by:**` or `**Dropped:**` when they apply, `**Created:**`, `**Updated:**`, `**Containers:**`, `**Addresses:**`, and `**Decision:**`, and the body sections `## Context`, `## Considered Options`, `## Decision Outcome`, and `## Consequences`; its `**Addresses:**` lists the `FR-nnn` and `QR-nn` it accounts for, with no relation word, and its status is one of `Proposed`, `Accepted`, `Superseded`, `Deprecated`, or `Dropped`.
- **Why:** MADR is the reference shape the course copies, so the sections are the ones the lecture's architectural decision record already uses.
  `**Addresses:**` is what makes the ASR a list rather than a field on the requirement, because a connection maintained on both sides goes stale on one.
  The five statuses separate a record whose decision is still being argued from one that has been superseded or dropped, which is what the ADR index has to sort.
- **Alternatives considered:** a single `docs/architecture/decisions.md` holding every record, rejected because each record must be linked and superseded on its own; and a status set of active and inactive only, rejected because a `Proposed` record and an `Accepted` one need different treatment in the index, and the week's three minimum records must be `Accepted` before submission.
- **Where it lands:** [Where Architectural Decision Records Live](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#where-architectural-decision-records-live), [The Record](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#the-record), and [What It Accounts For](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#what-it-accounts-for).

## A4-DEC-007: The Architecture Document Links Its Drivers, And The Vision Keeps The Scope

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/architecture/README.md` is the one architecture document: it opens with one sentence linking `docs/product-vision.md` as the scope it serves, then carries `## Drivers`, `## Container View` with `### Container Responsibilities`, `## Diagrams And Legends`, and `## Architectural Decisions` with the ADR index, and carries no trace section; the product vision keeps its goal, stakeholders, constraints, boundary, and system context with the embedded context diagram, and gains one `**Since: W4**` rule that it links the architecture document as a whole.
- **Why:** the constraints, boundary, and system context are the vision's own, and moving them would retarget every link the earlier weeks made.
  The drivers section points at the sections and files that hold the drivers, so the architecture document restates no goal, constraint, or requirement and the two documents cannot drift.
  The two links, one from each document, make the pair a scope and its response without either copying the other.
- **Alternatives considered:** moving the constraints, boundary, and system context into the architecture document, rejected because Week 2's rules own them and the Week 2 assignment stays historical; and having the architecture document restate the drivers it points at, rejected because a fact with two copies goes stale in one of them.
- **Where it lands:** [Where The Architecture Lives](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#where-the-architecture-lives), [Drivers](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#drivers), [Where The Vision Lives](../../requirements/product-vision-requirements.md#where-the-vision-lives), and [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-5-link-the-drivers-into-the-architecture).

## A4-DEC-008: The ADR Index Maps Each Driver To Its Record

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the ADR index is one table under `## Architectural Decisions` with the columns `ADR`, `Status`, `Addresses`, and `Decision`; `Proposed` and `Accepted` are active and the other three statuses are inactive; the active records come first in `ADR-nnn` order, and Week 4 requires the three minimum ADRs to be `Accepted` before submission.
- **Why:** the ADR index is the one place a driver meets the record that answers it, so a second table mapping drivers would be a copy that could disagree with it.
  The `Addresses` column names the `FR-nnn` and `QR-nn` of each record, which is how a reader walks from a requirement to the decision that shaped it.
  Requiring the minimum three to be `Accepted` is what keeps a decision still under discussion from counting as one the week actually made.
- **Alternatives considered:** a separate driver table beside the index, rejected because the `Addresses` column already carries the map and two tables would drift; and one `ADR-nnn` order with no active-first grouping, rejected because a reader would have to check every record's status to know whether it still stands.
- **Where it lands:** [Architectural Decisions](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#architectural-decisions) and [Part 6](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-6-record-the-architectural-decisions-and-their-index).

## A4-DEC-009: Story Points Stop At 13, And A Task Is Ordered By Priority

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `Story Points` is a public single-select issue field pinned to `User Story` with the options `1`, `2`, `3`, `5`, `8`, and `13`, and a story larger than `13SP` is split before it is estimated or scheduled; a task carries `Priority`, `High`, `Medium`, or `Low`, for its order and no estimate of its own, and ideal hours are not tracked.
- **Why:** the modified Fibonacci sequence is the one the reference and the course use, and a story that cannot be estimated inside it is a story too large to schedule.
  A task is a small unit whose order matters more than its size, so estimating every task would multiply the estimates without adding a decision the team uses.
  Ideal hours would be a fourth carrier that no total uses, since velocity is computed from the stories' points alone.
- **Alternatives considered:** estimating tasks as well as stories, rejected because velocity would then have two sources and a task's points would not be comparable with a story's; and leaving the sequence open above `13`, rejected because a story that large hides the work it contains and cannot be scheduled reliably.
- **Where it lands:** [Estimation](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#estimation), [Issue Fields](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#issue-fields), and [Part 1](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-1-plan-the-sprint-refine-the-backlog-and-set-the-fields).

## A4-DEC-010: Velocity Is Computed By Hand From The Story Points

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a sprint's `### SPRINT-nn` section carries `**Planned Velocity:**` and `**Actual Velocity:**` after `**Sprint Backlog:**`; planned velocity is the selected stories' points, set at planning, and actual velocity is the completed stories' points, set when the sprint closes, and both are computed by hand because a single-select field holds one option and cannot be added up.
- **Why:** the two fields are what let the team compare what it planned with what it finished, which is the point of holding the estimate at all.
  The value has to be computed by hand because the field holds one option, so no column can total it, and writing the computation into the rule stops a team from inventing a second estimate to get a total.
  The fields sit in the sprint section because that section is already the sprint's record and a second record of the same number would disagree with it.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `Velocity` document or a project column, rejected because a second record of the same number would drift; and a separate estimate kept as a number so a view could total it, rejected because it would be a second estimate beside `Story Points` and the two would disagree.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprint Planning](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#sprint-planning), [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints), and [Part 1](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-1-plan-the-sprint-refine-the-backlog-and-set-the-fields).

## A4-DEC-011: The Link Model Keeps One Fact On Each Side

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the ADR's `**Addresses:**` says which ASRs the decision accounts for, the FR's `**Traces to:**` says which ADRs constrain it, and there is no `ASR` field on any requirement; finding the ADRs that account for one requirement is a search of the ADR `**Addresses:**` lists.
- **Why:** the two facts are different — a record says it accounts for a requirement, and a requirement says what limits it — so neither is derived from the other and the ADR may be written before or after the FR.
  A connection maintained on both sides goes stale on one, which is the reason `A3-DEC-022` gives for its one-way chain.
  Making the ADR list the set of ASRs is what lets the reverse lookup be a search rather than a field that has to be kept in step.
- **Alternatives considered:** an `ASR: true` field on each requirement, rejected because the set of ASRs is exactly the union of the ADR lists and a second marker would have to match it; and a bidirectional link where a requirement also names its ADRs, rejected because the same connection would be maintained twice.
- **Where it lands:** [Architecturally Significant Requirements](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#architecturally-significant-requirements), [What It Accounts For](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#what-it-accounts-for), and [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks).

## A4-DEC-012: Issue Types Replace The Story And Task Labels

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the `User Story` and `Task` issue types are the marker for the two kinds of issue, they replace the `user-story` and `task` labels and are set on the existing issues in the same pass, and the six labels then retire once every issue carries its type and its `MoSCoW` value.
- **Why:** the three new fields pin to an issue type, so an issue's type has to be what says which kind of issue it is, and a label cannot be pinned to a field.
  A type is a platform property that filters and shows in the project view without a label to maintain, and the two kinds of issue already differ in their form, their fields, and their close states.
  Applying the type to the existing issues is what lets the labels go, because a repository half labelled and half typed would need both rules.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the labels beside the types, rejected because a label that duplicates a type becomes a second marker that can disagree with it; and creating the types without touching the existing issues, rejected because the labels could then never retire and the catch-up would have to happen anyway.
- **Where it lands:** [The Issue Form](../../requirements/user-stories-requirements.md#the-issue-form), [The Issue Form](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md#the-issue-form), [Issue Fields](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#issue-fields), and [Part 7](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-7-bring-the-existing-issues-to-the-week-4-shape).

## A4-DEC-013: MoSCoW Becomes An Issue Field

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `MoSCoW` is a public single-select issue field pinned to `User Story`, with the options `Must Have`, `Should Have`, `Could Have`, and `Won't Have`; the value is migrated from the four `moscow:*` labels, which then retire once every story carries the field.
- **Why:** priority is a typed, filterable value that the project view can show, while four labels are a flat namespace a reader cannot sort on and a form cannot set.
  Every story must carry the field before the labels go, so the one-time catch-up is what makes the retirement safe rather than a change that loses a priority.
  The field's options keep the meanings the story requirements already give the four priorities, so no meaning moves with the carrier.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the `moscow:*` labels, rejected because four labels for one field is the duplication the types remove; and letting both carriers run through the course, rejected because two sources for one priority disagree the first time a story is edited in one place.
- **Where it lands:** [Issue Fields](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#issue-fields) and [MoSCoW Prioritization](../../requirements/user-stories-requirements.md#moscow-prioritization), with the field in [Part 1](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-1-plan-the-sprint-refine-the-backlog-and-set-the-fields) and its migration in [Part 7](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-7-bring-the-existing-issues-to-the-week-4-shape).

## A4-DEC-014: A Story's Criteria Are Checkboxes The Accountable Member Ticks

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a story's acceptance criteria are checkboxes in the form `- [ ] AC-01: <criterion>`, and the form prefills the first one; the accountable member ticks a criterion once it is verified, a tick is state rather than a rewrite and needs no change comment, closing a story still follows the customer's verdict, and a closed story is reopened when a new related task is linked and then closed again.
- **Why:** a checkbox is where a reader can see which criteria are verified without reading the comments, and the tick belongs to the accountable member because that role answers for the criterion.
  A tick is state rather than a rewrite, so it needs no change comment that would bury the story's decisions.
  Closing still follows the customer, so a ticked criterion is evidence rather than acceptance, and reopening is the only way to add the link a new task needs without rewriting the story's history.
- **Alternatives considered:** a bullet list with the criteria unchanged, rejected because nothing would record which one had been verified; and a change comment on every tick, rejected because a state that changes several times per story would drown the story's decisions.
- **Where it lands:** [Acceptance Criteria](../../requirements/user-stories-requirements.md#acceptance-criteria), [The Issue Form](../../requirements/user-stories-requirements.md#the-issue-form), and [Part 7](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-7-bring-the-existing-issues-to-the-week-4-shape).

## A4-DEC-015: An AC-nn Also Belongs To A Functional Requirement

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** an `AC-nn` may belong to an FR as well as to a story or task issue, and an FR's `## Acceptance Criteria` holds one `### AC-nn` section per criterion, so each criterion has an anchor and can be cited as `FR-012 AC-02` and linked as `FR-012.md#ac-02`.
- **Why:** the FR has to be checkable without opening a story, and a criterion that cannot be linked cannot be cited by the task or pull request that advances it.
  Giving each criterion its own section is what produces the anchor, because an inline list would leave nothing to link to.
  Extending the existing family rather than issuing a second one keeps one meaning for `AC-nn` across issues and requirement files.
- **Alternatives considered:** a second family for an FR's criteria, rejected because the course would then have two kinds of criterion and one citation form would be ambiguous; and a plain table of criteria in the FR, rejected because a row has no stable anchor to link.
- **Where it lands:** [Acceptance Criteria](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#acceptance-criteria) and [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules).

## A4-DEC-016: The Ceremonies Come From The Scrum Guide

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the sprint ceremonies follow the [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html): sprint planning opens a sprint, product backlog refinement prepares the next two sprints, estimation is an activity inside planning and refinement rather than a ceremony, the weekly customer meeting is the Sprint Review, and every sprint from `SPRINT-04` ends with a retrospective.
- **Why:** the lecture teaches quality and architecture and its Scrum slides are commented out, so the course has no source material of its own for the ceremonies and the guide is the method's own statement.
  Mapping the weekly customer meeting to the Sprint Review keeps one meeting instead of adding a second, which the review's own rule states when it says the review is not a second meeting.
  Treating estimation as an activity rather than a ceremony is why three issue fields carry it and no separate meeting is required.
- **Alternatives considered:** teaching the ceremonies from the deck, rejected because the material is not there and uncommenting it would change a built lecture for one week; and adding a separate Sprint Review meeting, rejected because the weekly customer meeting already exists and its report is the record.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprint Planning](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#sprint-planning), [Product Backlog Refinement](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#product-backlog-refinement), [Estimation](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#estimation), and [Sprint Review](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#sprint-review).

## A4-DEC-017: The Retrospective Has Four Sections And Carried Actions

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the sprint retrospective at `reports/week-NN/sprint-retrospective.md` carries `What Went Well`, `What Did Not`, `Actions`, and `Carried Actions`, with a `Reason` on every action and an `Outcome` on every carried action; the first file, `SPRINT-04`'s, says there is no previous retrospective instead of carrying an empty table, and the weekly report's coverage table links the file.
- **Why:** the four sections are what let one sprint's action be followed into the next, which is the point of holding the retrospective at all.
  A `Reason` column is what keeps an action from being a wish, and the `Carried Actions` outcomes are what show whether the last sprint's actions were done.
  The first sprint has no previous file, so saying so is honest where an empty table would look like a lost record, and the coverage row puts the file in the week's submission without the report restating it.
- **Alternatives considered:** a three-question retrospective with no carried section, the 2025 shape, rejected because it let an action disappear between sprints; and a retrospective section inside the weekly report, rejected because the plan, the issues, and the retrospective file own those records and the report links the file instead.
- **Where it lands:** [Retrospective](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#retrospective), [Weekly Public Report](../../requirements/weekly-report-requirements.md#weekly-public-report), and [Part 12](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-12-close-the-sprint-and-hold-the-retrospective).

## A4-DEC-018: A Task's Requirements Section Names Its FRs And ADRs

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the task issue form gains a `### Requirements` section naming the `FR-nnn` and `ADR-nnn` the task works on, as links on `main`; a task that names only an ADR reaches the threshold through that ADR's `**Addresses:**`, and a task that serves no requirement says so and is repository upkeep rather than product work.
- **Why:** a task is the unit a pull request closes, so a requirement named there is one hop from the change rather than two, which is what lets a reader walk from a line of code to the requirement it serves.
  Keeping the task's `### Story` section beside it means the task still says which `AC-nn` it advances, while the new section says what it builds, so the two links answer different questions.
  Naming only an ADR is allowed because the record already lists the FRs and QRs it accounts for, so the shorter route does not break the chain.
- **Alternatives considered:** naming only stories on a task, rejected because a task that implements an architectural decision would then reach no FR; and requiring both an FR and an ADR on every task, rejected because a task may be repository upkeep, and a forced link would make the trace wrong rather than complete.
- **Where it lands:** [The Issue Form](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md#the-issue-form), [Where Task Issues Live](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md#where-task-issues-live), [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks), and [Part 7](../../assignments/assignment-4.md#part-7-bring-the-existing-issues-to-the-week-4-shape).

## A4-DEC-019: The Mandated Testability Requirement Is High Business Importance

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the quality requirement the course mandates — the `Maintainability` / `Testability` one that names its critical modules by repository path — carries `**Business importance:** High`, and the rule states the reason so the priority matrix cannot rate it `Low`.
- **Why:** the course is part of the business and the instructors are stakeholders who require the requirement, so its business importance is settled by the course rather than by the team's judgement.
  Testability is the attribute a matrix is most tempted to bury, because the team pays for it and the customer does not see it.
  Naming the reason in the rule is what makes the rating checkable instead of an unexplained `High` that a team could change.
- **Alternatives considered:** letting the team rate it like any other requirement, rejected because the matrix would then be free to sort the course's own requirement last; and mandating the rating without a reason, rejected because an unexplained `High` invites the team to edit it away.

## A4-DEC-031: No Fallback Exists For Issue Types Or Fields

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the two issue types and the five fields — `Responsible`, `Accountable`, `MoSCoW`, `Story Points`, and `Priority` — are the only carriers of what they record; a team whose organization cannot create issue types or fields declares a deviation, and no substitute is defined, so the Week 3 `responsible:` and `accountable:` label fallback is removed with them.
- **Why:** a fallback turns one rule into two, and the second one is never as complete as the first, which is how a repository ends up with a label that carries a value the project view cannot show.
  A deviation is the course's own way of recording that something was done differently, per [Declaring Deviations](../../requirements/weekly-report-requirements.md#declaring-deviations), so the team still has a place to say what happened and why.
  Removing the Week 3 fallback is what keeps one rule instead of a rule and a remembered exception.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the `responsible:` and `accountable:` label fallback and adding a label for each new value, rejected because six labels and a field for the same fact is the duplication the types remove; and defining a label fallback for the three new fields, rejected because it would double every rule and hide the deviation the course wants recorded.
- **Where it lands:** [Issue Fields](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#issue-fields), [Scheduling An Issue](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#scheduling-an-issue), and [Declaring Deviations](../../requirements/weekly-report-requirements.md#declaring-deviations).
