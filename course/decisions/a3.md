# Assignment 3 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignment 3 and the requirements it depends on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignment and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A3-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the week's scope to the vocabulary it settles.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
Mechanical edits — dates the syllabus owns, routing, backlog housekeeping — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A3-DEC-001: Week 3 Is Planning Plus The First Working Slice

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 3 makes the plan explicit and ships the first working slice of the product: the threshold of success, the plan, the risk register, and the Definition of Done, then the candidate built on `main`, shown to the customer, and released.
- **Why:** the syllabus names both halves as the week's deliverables, and the Week 3 lecture teaches strategic and tactical planning, so the week applies the concepts and gives the product its first code.
  Keeping the planning and the delivery in one week is what lets the plan point at something built and the build point back at a goal.
- **Alternatives considered:** planning only, which would leave the customer's accepted candidate unbuilt for another week; and product only, which would move the planning concepts to a week the syllabus does not give them.
- **Where it lands:** [`assignments/assignment-3.md`](../../assignments/assignment-3.md), with the week's deliverables in [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-3-oct-9--oct-15-planning--minimum-usable-product) and the concepts it applies in [the Week 3 lecture](../../lectures/lecture-3.typ).

## A3-DEC-002: The Sprint Structure Lands In Week 3, The Ceremonies In Week 4

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 3 introduces the sprint as the plan's timebox and the week's release cycle — seven `SPRINT-nn` sections, seven GitHub milestones, and one release per sprint — and leaves sprint planning, grooming, estimation, story points, and the retrospective to Week 4.
- **Why:** the plan and the scheduling model need the timebox from the week the plan is written, but the ceremonies are the part of Scrum the course has no source material for, and Week 3 already carries the first code, the first release, four artifacts, the docs site, and the issue fields.
- **Alternatives considered:** teaching estimation with planning in Week 3, where the 2025 lecture had it; and introducing the ceremonies without the structure, which would run them against a plan that does not exist yet.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints) and [Scheduling An Issue](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#scheduling-an-issue) in Week 3; the ceremonies arrive with the Week 4 materials.

## A3-DEC-003: The Plan Is `docs/project-plan.md`, Milestones Then Sprints

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the maintained plan is `docs/project-plan.md`, and it reads `## Milestones` — the path to the threshold — then `## Sprints`, each with its nested `#### REL-nn` release, then `## Progress Monitoring`, as sections rather than index tables.
- **Why:** the name has to carry more than a roadmap, because the file also holds the increments and progress monitoring, and it has to match the register of `docs/product-vision.md`; the parts read from the path to the threshold down to the weeks that walk it; and the sections themselves carry the dates and the releases, so index tables only restated them.
- **Alternatives considered:** `roadmap`, because it is narrower than the file; `work-plan`, because it reads like the week's tasks; `plan.md`, the 2025 name, because it is too generic; and the two index tables for sprints and milestones, dropped because each section carries `**Due:**` and its nested release, or GitHub carries the date on the sprint's milestone.
- **Where it lands:** [Where The Plan And The Threshold Live](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#where-the-plan-and-the-threshold-live), [Milestones](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#milestones), and [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints).

## A3-DEC-004: Sprint Sections Carry A Goal, A Due Date, And A Backlog Link

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a `### SPRINT-nn` section opens with the sprint's goal in one sentence, followed by `**Due:**` and, once the sprint has issues, `**Sprint Backlog:**`, a link to the sprint's GitHub milestone; all seven sprints are written in Week 3, and a sprint not yet planned carries a provisional goal kept current until it is planned.
- **Why:** every identifier's section needs a stable anchor and a first line a reader can stop at, and the goal answers what will be true at the sprint's end; the issue list already exists on GitHub, so the plan links it instead of copying it; and a provisional goal keeps a future week from standing empty in the plan.
- **Alternatives considered:** a saved list of issues in the plan, rejected because the milestone page is always current and the plan would go stale; and leaving future sprints blank, rejected because the plan must still give the week a direction.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints) and [Give Every Unplanned Sprint A Provisional Goal](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#give-every-unplanned-sprint-a-provisional-goal).

## A3-DEC-005: Plan Milestones Are Increments, And Only The MUP One Is Required

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a plan milestone (`MLS-nn`) is an increment on the path to the threshold, due at the end of its week; it is documentation only — no GitHub milestone, no state, no check — and Week 3 requires exactly one, `MLS-03`, whose focus is delivering the MUP; further milestones are the team's choice, numbered by their due week, and the `**Sprints:**` runs are disjoint, consecutive, and end in the week their milestone is due.
- **Why:** the weekly releases give the cadence, and the milestones give the increments the cadence cannot express; requiring more than `MLS-03` would make teams invent increments they cannot see yet; a `**Done when:**` check had neither a state nor a place to record a slip, because a sprint's GitHub milestone always closes with its release; and numbering by due week keeps the number meaningful, with a week without a milestone leaving a gap that the identifier rules expect.
- **Alternatives considered:** one plan milestone per week, which restated the sprints; a milestone set whose runs together named every sprint, which forced one milestone per sprint; and the `**Done when:**` field, dropped because it promised a check that nothing kept.
- **Where it lands:** [Milestones](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#milestones) and [Lead The Description With The Outcome](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#lead-the-description-with-the-outcome), with the candidate's core task as `MLS-03`'s first sentence in [Where The Candidate Lives](../../requirements/minimum-usable-product-requirements.md#where-the-candidate-lives).

## A3-DEC-006: A Sprint Is The GitHub Milestone, And Scheduling Is One Act

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** an issue is scheduled by assigning it to its sprint's GitHub milestone and, in the same act, setting the `Responsible` and `Accountable` values and both members as the assignees; there is no `Sprint` issue field, and no parking milestone or parked value, so unscheduled work is exactly the open issues that sit in no milestone, and the seven sprint milestones are created when the plan is written, so scheduling is assigning a milestone that already exists.
- **Why:** the milestone is set when an issue is planned anyway, it lists its issues directly, and it needs no option list to maintain, while a `Sprint` field duplicated the schedule and could disagree with it; an unscheduled issue is already visible as one in no milestone, so a parking value added nothing; and creating the milestones with the plan is what makes the plan the schedule, because an issue never waits on a milestone to exist.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `Sprint` single-select issue field, replaced by the milestone; and a parking milestone with a parked value, replaced by the absence of a milestone.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints), [Scheduling An Issue](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#scheduling-an-issue), and [Schedule With The Milestone, Not In The File](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#schedule-with-the-milestone-not-in-the-file).

## A3-DEC-007: One Release Per Sprint, Tagged Before Anything Links It

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** every submission week from Week 3 ends in one release, `REL-nn` after its sprint, carried as a `#### REL-nn` section nested in the sprint; the tag is pushed on the state after the customer meeting and any same-week rework, and only then do the changelog's released section, the plan's `REL-nn` line, and the week's report merge; and the sprint's GitHub milestone closes once its release is cut, after any issue still open is rescheduled into a later sprint's milestone.
- **Why:** the weekly cadence gives every sprint a stable, citable state without inventing a second version sequence beside SemVer; tagging before the documents that link the release means every link resolves the first time it is checked; the changelog stays the user-facing record while the plan stays the week's record, so the two link the release instead of each other; and the milestone's state follows the release, so the sprint's page is what it shipped, while a slip moves the leftover issue into a later sprint rather than extending the run past its due week.
- **Alternatives considered:** fixing a version number per week, rejected because the numbering stays the team's choice under SemVer; linking the changelog from the plan, rejected because the changelog's released section already links the release and dated headings make fragile anchors; and a second release per tag with an alternative nickname, rejected because GitHub refuses a second release for a tag, so the title repeats the tag and the nickname goes in the body.
- **Where it lands:** [Releases](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#releases), [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release), and [Versioning And Tags](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#versioning-and-tags).

## A3-DEC-008: The Threshold Of Success Keeps The Statements It Came From

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/threshold-of-success.md` holds the minimal SMART set as `## TOS-nn` sections and, below them, a growth-only table of the failure and success statements the set came from; a goal's change or drop is agreed with the customer and cites the customer's `DEC-nnn`; and the product vision's `## Goal` links the threshold, with no link back.
- **Why:** the lecture defines the threshold as the minimum set of goals that must all be achieved, so the set has to stay small and checkable; keeping the statements shows which fears became goals, which were merged, and which were left to the roadmap, so the bar cannot be edited into looking chosen from nothing; and one link, from the vision's goal, keeps the two files from drifting, with the plan reaching the threshold through each milestone's `**Advances:**`.
- **Alternatives considered:** a statements table kept current or frozen at the first session, both replaced by growth-only, because a goal added later must still trace back to its statement while the words the session wrote are the evidence; and moving or deleting a `Missed` goal, rejected because the threshold is only honest if its failures stay in it.
- **Where it lands:** [The Threshold Of Success](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#the-threshold-of-success), [Goal](../../requirements/product-vision-requirements.md#goal), and [Step 1: Agree On The Threshold Of Success](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#step-1-agree-on-the-threshold-of-success).

## A3-DEC-009: The Risk Register Owns Contingency, And Review Is The Team's Own

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/risks.md` is the single home of contingency planning: each `## RISK-nn` states cause, event, and impact in one sentence, then carries `Status`, `Impact`, `Likelihood`, `Trigger`, `Window`, `Mitigation`, and `Contingency`; several fallbacks are several risks; a point of no return is a `DEC-nnn`; and the team reads the register and the threshold at its own weekly review, not at the customer meeting.
- **Why:** the lecture's contingency planning asks for the alternative solution, and two homes for it would drift; a risk with a choice inside its fallback is a decision the team has not made, so several fallbacks cannot share one entry; a point of no return is already a decision trigger, so the risks and the plan can cite the decision log; and the only meeting the course requires is the weekly customer meeting, which is already full, so the review cadence has to be the team's own arrangement, stated in the plan to be checkable.
- **Alternatives considered:** an `**Owner:**` field, rejected because a task issue names a responsible member; a `**Milestone:**` field, rejected because the plan already places the risk's window; and a `Point of no return` field, rejected because it is a decision.
- **Where it lands:** [Where The Risks Live](../../requirements/risks-requirements.md#where-the-risks-live), [Response](../../requirements/risks-requirements.md#response), [Progress Monitoring](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#progress-monitoring), and [The Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#the-decision).

## A3-DEC-010: The Definition Of Done Is A Dedicated Artifact With Three Lists

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/definition-of-done.md` states what done means for a story, a task, and a pull request as a `Required` minimum of plain bullets in three disjoint sections; `CONTRIBUTING.md` links it rather than restating it; and a team whose in-repository tracker has its own Definition of Done states where that list lives, without tying the two lists together.
- **Why:** the lecture teaches a Definition of Done, but the tracker's list is only `Recommended` and covers tracker tasks, not the repository's units; disjoint lists keep each item under the smallest unit it applies to, so nothing is said twice; and plain bullets are right because a committed list is not ticked per pull request.
- **Alternatives considered:** checkboxes per pull request, rejected because the same list would read as a changing state; and extending the tracker's Definition of Done, rejected because it answers a different question.
- **Where it lands:** [The Story](../../requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md#the-story), [The Task](../../requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md#the-task), [The Pull Request](../../requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md#the-pull-request), and [Contributing](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#contributing).

## A3-DEC-011: Week 3 Introduces Five Identifier Families

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 3 adds `RISK-nn`, `TOS-nn`, `SPRINT-nn`, `MLS-nn`, and `REL-nn`, all zero-padded and never renumbered or reused; `RISK` and `TOS` follow the ordinary sectioned-identifier rules, while `SPRINT`, `MLS`, and `REL` are exceptions to the `**Status:**` rule because the sprint's GitHub milestone and the release carry their own state and a plan milestone carries none, and `MLS` and `REL` are also exceptions to the first-line rule.
- **Why:** the plan needs stable targets a later week can cite: a sprint section, a milestone that maps to the threshold, and a release the reports and the changelog point at; and a state field would be a copy that could disagree with the platform object, which is why the exceptions go where the platform already keeps the state.
- **Alternatives considered:** `UP-nn` for the next increment, dropped before it was used; and `SPRINT-??` for an unplanned sprint, dropped once all seven sprints were numbered from the start.
- **Where it lands:** [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules) and [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks).

## A3-DEC-012: Roles Are Public Issue Fields, Mirrored By The Assignees And The Project

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the organization creates two single-select `Public` issue fields, `Responsible` and `Accountable`, pinned to the issue types the team uses; a scheduled issue's assignees are exactly the two members its values name; and every team runs one public GitHub project whose table view shows the two fields, the built-in `Assignees`, and the built-in `Milestone`, grouped or sorted by `Milestone`.
  A team whose organization cannot create issue fields declares a deviation and carries the two roles as labels instead.
- **Why:** issue fields are the typed, filterable way to name the roles, and a public project shows only issue fields whose visibility is `Public`, so the fields have to be `Public` for a reader outside the repository to see them; the assignees stay because they notify, filter, and appear in the project without opening an issue; and the two roles must differ, which is the no-self-approval rule at the level of an issue.
- **Alternatives considered:** carrying the roles in the issue form, rejected because the values are set when the issue is scheduled, after creation, so no form change or catch-up pull request is needed; and a private project, rejected because it would show nobody the schedule.
- **Where it lands:** [Issue Fields](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#issue-fields), [Assignees](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#assignees), and [GitHub Projects](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#github-projects).

## A3-DEC-013: The MUP Is Built On `main`, Shown Criterion By Criterion, And Released

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 3's build scope is exactly the Week 2 candidate; its stories are built into one working slice on `main` through task issues and one pull request per task, each merged before the customer meeting; "working" means a clean checkout completes the core task end to end by following `docs/setup.md`; each finished story is shown against its `AC-nn` with the verdict recorded as a `DEC-nnn`; a candidate story the team will not finish moves out only with the customer's decision, which also re-scopes the core task by editing the first sentence of the active milestone's description; and the sprint's release is cut after the meeting and any same-week rework.
- **Why:** the candidate is the scope the customer accepted, so the week owes exactly it and a `Must Have` story outside it is not owed; task issues and one pull request per task are what make each change traceable from a line of code to a story and a goal; the acceptance criteria are the customer's yes-or-no test, so the verdict is a decision; and a move-out changes what the product is, so only the customer can make it, while the plan records the new scope where the increment is described.
- **Alternatives considered:** leaving work on a branch for a partial demonstration, rejected because the slice would not be delivered; and the team deciding a move-out, rejected because it would narrow the accepted product without the customer.
- **Where it lands:** [Building, Delivering, And Releasing The Candidate](../../requirements/minimum-usable-product-requirements.md#building-delivering-and-releasing-the-candidate), [Showing Working Software](../../requirements/customer-meetings-requirements.md#showing-working-software), and [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-3.md#part-5-build-the-minimum-usable-product).

## A3-DEC-014: The Stack Is A Decision, And There Is No Functional-Requirements File

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the stack chosen in Week 3 is recorded as a `DEC-nnn` entry, `.gitignore` is extended for it, and its package ecosystem joins `github-actions` in `.github/dependabot.yml`; stories with their acceptance criteria remain the functional requirements, and no `docs/functional-requirements.md` is created.
- **Why:** a technology choice already requires a decision entry, and Week 4 may revise the stack once the architecture is chosen, which is then another decision; a second list of requirements would contradict the rule that stories are the only one, and scope and out-of-scope already live in the vision's boundary.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `docs/functional-requirements.md` collected from stories and constraints, deferred; and treating an actor or flow view as a requirement document, left to Week 4's architecture work.
- **Where it lands:** [The Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#the-decision), [Repository Setup](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#repository-setup) and [Contributing](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#contributing) for the stack's files and its dependency bumps, and [Part 3](../../assignments/assignment-3.md#part-3-decide-the-stack).

## A3-DEC-015: The Docs Site Serves `docs/`, And `docs/setup.md` Is The One Run Path

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** every team publishes `docs/` as a GitHub Pages site through an Actions workflow with pinned actions, and links the site from the root `README.md`; any static site generator is allowed, and the site's default page is a landing page; `docs/setup.md` holds the setup and run instructions as a content minimum that the README links, and the MUP's "working" definition follows it; the site does not link the weekly reports, and a page that links a file outside `docs/` uses that file's GitHub URL.
- **Why:** the site is the product's front door online, and a default page that is a file listing gives a visitor nowhere to go; one canonical copy of the run instructions serves both the site and the repository while the README stays the repository's front door; the MUP needs a run path a stranger can follow, which makes the setup page part of what "working" means; and the two link rules keep the site a view of the maintained documentation, with the reports left to the repository and every outside link resolving on the site and in the repository alike.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the run instructions in the root README, rejected because the site could not serve them without a second copy; and requiring one generator, rejected so teams can choose, with `mdBook`, `Zensical`, and `MkDocs` as the examples, best maintained first.
- **Where it lands:** [Documentation Site](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#documentation-site), [Setup And Run Instructions](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#setup-and-run-instructions), and [Root README](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#root-readme).

## A3-DEC-016: The Method Lives In The Planning And Tracking Guide

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a new `guides/planning-and-tracking.md` carries the method — the threshold's five steps, the roadmap's zoom level, the milestones, the sprints and their releases, the risks and fallbacks, progress monitoring, and the review-and-adjust loop — including how to lead a milestone's description with its outcome.
- **Why:** the course's layers put rules in `requirements/` and method in `guides/`, and students read a lecture once; the threshold process and the milestone-description framing would otherwise have no home, because a requirements file owns rules, not steps.
- **Alternatives considered:** teaching the method from the lecture deck and the rules alone, rejected because the deck alone cannot carry it; and including estimation in the guide, left out because the ceremonies arrive in Week 4.
- **Where it lands:** [Guide: Planning And Tracking](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md), linked from [Planning Requirements](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md) and [Risk Requirements](../../requirements/risks-requirements.md).

## A3-DEC-017: The New Requirements Files Each Own One Artifact Group

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the plan and the threshold are owned by `requirements/planning-requirements.md`, the register by `requirements/risks-requirements.md`, and the Definition of Done by `requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md`; the same work gave the issue rules and the pull request lifecycle their own files, out of `repository-requirements.md`.
- **Why:** the plan and the threshold are one group, as the Week 1 research files are; the risks and the Definition of Done have their own lifecycles, fields, and citation rules; and issue tracking and pull requests had each grown past what one repository file should own, so splitting them kept one owner per rule, with every router naming the new files as the repository's routing rule requires.
- **Alternatives considered:** extending the existing artifact-group file with the new artifacts, rejected because each new file's citation links differ from the group's.
- **Where it lands:** [Planning Requirements](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md), [Risk Requirements](../../requirements/risks-requirements.md), [Definition Of Done Requirements](../../requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md), [Issue Tracking Requirements](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md), and [Pull Request Requirements](../../requirements/pull-requests-requirements.md).

## A3-DEC-018: The Vocabulary And The Deferrals Follow The Week That Teaches Them

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the syllabus renames the work plan to the project plan and delivers "the next product release" where it said "Improved MUP", keeping `MVP` only as vocabulary; the product-code CI moves to Week 4 and the production deployment stays in Week 5, so Week 3 ships its first release without an automated product check.
- **Why:** the syllabus is the single source of truth for the schedule, and a stage name the course no longer delivers would imply a deliverable that does not exist; automatic checking arrives with the quality and verification lecture, so the week that builds and releases does not also carry a check the students have not been taught yet.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the product-code CI in Week 3, moved once its teaching week was settled; and keeping the MVP-stage names in the syllabus, replaced by named releases (`REL-04` to `REL-09`) with `MVP` left to the vocabulary and the prototype requirements.
- **Where it lands:** [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-3-oct-9--oct-15-planning--minimum-usable-product), [Continuous Integration](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#continuous-integration) with its `**Since: W4**` marker, and [Release Requirements](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md).

## A3-DEC-019: The Course Suggests Tools, It Does Not Require Them

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `course/tooling.md` lists the tools the course suggests — AssemblyAI for meeting transcripts, Google AI Studio for early prototyping, and OpenCode as a coding agent — each with a line on what it is for and a link to the requirements that own the work it touches; the page is a course document, so the tools are suggestions rather than rules, and Assignment 3 points at it once.
- **Why:** the rules care about the artifact and not the product that produced it, so a named tool would become an obligation the course does not mean to make; a short list gives a team a starting point and keeps the choice; and putting each suggestion beside the rule it serves shows where the tool's output lands.
- **Alternatives considered:** putting the tools in `requirements/`, rejected because a requirement would make one product obligatory; and prescribing one tool per job, rejected because the rules do not care which product produces the artifact.
- **Where it lands:** [Course Tooling](../tooling.md), the Course Documents table in [the README](../../README.md#course-documents), and the intro of [Assignment 3](../../assignments/assignment-3.md).

## A3-DEC-020: The Weekly Report Carries The Meeting's Verdicts

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the weekly public report states the week's customer-meeting outcome — each story shown and whether the customer accepted or rejected it, and each candidate story that moved out — each citing the `DEC-nnn` that records it.
- **Why:** the verdict is taken in a meeting whose report belongs to the team, while the weekly public report is what a reader outside the issues can check, so the outcome has to survive where the evidence is; and citing the `DEC-nnn` keeps the verdict one hop from the decision instead of retelling it.
- **Alternatives considered:** leaving the outcome to the meeting report alone, rejected because a reader would have to reconstruct it from the meeting report and the issue comments; and a summary without citations, rejected because a verdict would not be traceable to the decision behind it.
- **Where it lands:** [Weekly Public Report](../../requirements/weekly-report-requirements.md#weekly-public-report) rule 12, and the report part of [Assignment 3](../../assignments/assignment-3.md#assignment-report-in-the-repository).

## A3-DEC-021: The Assignment Is A Delta Ordered By The Order Of Work

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Assignment 3 is written as ten parts in the order the work can succeed — the threshold, the plan and the risks, the stack, done and the workflow, the MUP, the docs site and the setup page, the Week 2 follow-ups, the customer meeting, the release, and the AI usage report — each part linking the rules that own what it asks for and adding only the week's paths, numbers, and minima: at least 3 goals, at least 3 risks, seven sprint sections with Week 3 and Week 4 concrete, the seven `SPRINT-nn` GitHub milestones, `MLS-03`, every issue the plan schedules in its sprint's milestone with the two values and the assignees, and the first release tagged.
- **Why:** the assignment is a delta against the requirements rather than a second rulebook, so a reader can work top to bottom without meeting a rule twice; ordering the parts by the order of work makes each part depend only on the parts above it; and the minima make the week checkable by the team and by a grader.
- **Alternatives considered:** ordering by artifact type, rejected because it would put the release before the meeting that has to happen first; and restating the rules inside the assignment, rejected because a rule with two owners drifts.
- **Where it lands:** [Assignment 3](../../assignments/assignment-3.md), with the week's deliverables in [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-3-oct-9--oct-15-planning--minimum-usable-product).

## A3-DEC-022: The Chain From A Commit To The Threshold

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a line of code reaches the threshold in one chain: the commit that introduced it belongs to a pull request that closes one task issue; the task's `Story` field names the story and the `AC-nn` it works toward, the story cites its `VP-nn` and its origins in `Traces to`, and the issue sits in its sprint's GitHub milestone; the plan's `SPRINT-nn` section links that milestone, the `MLS-nn` whose `**Sprints:**` names it `**Advances:**` a `TOS-nn`, and the threshold refines the goal of the vision; and every weekly report from Week 3 cites its `REL-nn`.
- **Why:** the course's goal is that any artifact, down to a line of code, can be answered with why it exists, and the chain is what lets a reader walk from a commit up to the goal in the threshold of success; and the links point one way, from the later artifact to the earlier identifier, because a connection maintained in both directions goes stale on one side, while the reverse direction is found by search.
- **Alternatives considered:** bidirectional links or a reverse index, rejected because the same connection would be maintained twice; and a single document restating the chain, rejected because stories with their acceptance criteria are the only requirements list.
- **Where it lands:** the Week 3 rows of [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks), and the rules the chain passes through in [Planning Requirements](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md), [Issue Tracking Requirements](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md), [Task Issue Requirements](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md), [User Story Requirements](../../requirements/user-stories-requirements.md), and [Release Requirements](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md).
