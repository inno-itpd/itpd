# Assignment 9 And 10 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignments 9 and 10 and the requirements they depend on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignments and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A9-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the two-sprint scope to the order the work is submitted in.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
An entry that reverses an earlier `An-DEC` records the whole reversal and restates the clauses that survive, because a reversal cannot be partial.
Mechanical edits — backlog housekeeping and the dates a decision here sets — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A9-DEC-001: Two Sprints, Two Submissions, And How The Reviews Are Scheduled

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `SPRINT-09` is Week 9 and Submission 9 is due `2026-11-29`; Week 9's class dates stay 20–26 November, and the Sprint 9 review may be held by the sprint's due date of `2026-11-29`, including the days after the class week.
  `SPRINT-10` starts `2026-11-30` and is due `2026-12-06`; Submission 10 is due `2026-12-06`.
  Submission 10 has a hard deadline of Sunday 6 December 2026 at 23:59.
  Moodle accepts no late submission for Assignment 10.
  `SPRINT-10`'s milestone and provisional section are created in Week 9, and it is planned in Week 10.
  The Sprint 10 review and handoff are one in-person meeting by `2026-12-06`.
  The customer attends the handoff.
  The extra days after the Week 10 class window give teams time to complete the handoff, release, and final report.
  The final exam is `2026-12-04` in the syllabus's Week 11 section, and the individual reflection and peer evaluation is Submission 11, due `2026-12-05`, in that section.
  Sprint 10 closes after `REL-10`.
  Product code freezes at `REL-10`, and the public repository freezes at the Submission 10 deadline.
  The assignment catalog separates the ten group submissions from the final exam, Submission 11, and attendance, so no item number competes with a submission number.
- **Why:** the course grades Week 10 as well as Week 9, and one course window is one sprint with its own submission; a single final submission cannot carry the Sprint 9 review, the UAT run, and the Week 10 handoff.
  Week 9's class dates do not move, so the sprint and its review extend to the submission deadline instead of a second meeting in the class week.
- **Alternatives considered:** one final submission with Week 9 folded into Week 10, rejected because the Sprint 9 review and its UAT run would lose their record; a Week 10 with no submission, rejected because the final state and the final report would have no deadline; forcing the Sprint 9 review into the 20–26 November class week, rejected because the sprint's work continues to the 29 November deadline.
- **Where it lands:** [the plan](../plans/a9-plan.md#design-decisions), [the syllabus](../syllabus.md), `assignments/assignment-9.md`, and `assignments/assignment-10.md`.

## A9-DEC-003: The Handoff Is The Sprint 10 Review And `reports/week-10/handoff.md`

## A9-DEC-005: The Assessment Is Ten Group Submissions, 107 Points, And 75 To Pass

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the course has ten group submissions at 7 points each (70), 10 points of attendance, 7 points of individual reflection and peer evaluation (Submission 11), and 20 points of final exam, for 107 points; the pass threshold is 75 points of the 107; the late penalty is 10% of the submission's points per calendar day, more than 7 days late receives zero, and a 7-point submission loses 0.7 points per day; repository upkeep is assessed inside the ten submissions; Submission 9 is due `2026-11-29`; Submission 11 is due `2026-12-05` and is available only to a member of a group that presented at the final exam.
  The late penalty applies to submissions that accept late work.
  Assignment 10 has a hard cutoff and accepts no late submission.
  The assignment catalog carries the ten group submissions in the assignment table, and the final exam, Submission 11, and attendance in a separate non-assignment section.
  The weight shift from the old percentages is deliberate: the group submissions carry 70 of the 107 points, attendance 10, the individual reflection 7, and the final exam 20.
- **Why:** the course grades Week 10, so it has a tenth group submission; points keep the arithmetic visible and the late penalty proportional to the submission; 75 points of the 107 is the maintainer's chosen threshold; the catalog split keeps the submission numbers and the assessment-item numbers from competing.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping percentages, rejected because 107 points does not divide cleanly into 100%; reading the pass as 75% of 107 (80.25 points), rejected because the maintainer set 75 points; a separate repository-upkeep component, rejected because upkeep is assessed inside the submissions; keeping item numbers on the final exam and Submission 11, rejected because they are not assignments.
- **Where it lands:** [the syllabus](../syllabus.md) and [the plan](../plans/a9-plan.md#design-decisions).

## A9-DEC-006: The Final Sprint Has No Later Sprint

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** at the Sprint 9 close, only issues necessary to make the UATs pass and selected documentation work may move to `SPRINT-10`; other open issues remain unscheduled, outside sprint and plan milestones; at the Sprint 10 close, the completed issues stay in the closed milestone, and every open issue, including the ones scheduled and not completed, leaves the milestone, stays open, clears its `Responsible`, `Accountable`, and assignees, and is listed in `reports/week-10/handoff.md`; the final retrospective says there is no later sprint, a `Not done` action is `Outcome: Not done` in `## Carried Actions`, and the `## Actions` carry `Carried: No`; the project reflection is independent of the retrospective's actions.
  The final-sprint exception overrides retrospective rule 4: a `Not done` action is not re-listed in `## Actions`, because there is no next retrospective.
- **Why:** there is no later sprint to receive an open issue or a carried action; an open issue in a closed milestone would claim work the sprint did not finish; the retrospective's carried-action table already has the `Outcome` value.
- **Alternatives considered:** leaving open issues in the closed milestone, rejected because the milestone would be a false claim; carrying the retrospective actions into the reflection, rejected because the reflection is the project's lessons; re-listing a not-done action in `## Actions`, rejected because the carried-action table already owns it and there is no next sprint.
- **Where it lands:** [Planning Requirements](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints), [Issue Tracking Requirements](../../requirements/issue-tracking-requirements.md#scheduling-an-issue), and [Sprint Ceremonies Requirements](../../requirements/sprint-ceremonies-requirements.md#retrospective).

- **Why:** deletion is mandatory so the platform cannot leak the data; the main repository is a course-wide public artifact; privatisation after grading needs the instructors' consent because it changes who can open the snapshot; one owner per rule keeps the two files from carrying a second copy; the customer should not have to hunt earlier wrappers for credentials.
- **Alternatives considered:** optional deletion, rejected because the platform risk is the reason to delete; transferring the private data, rejected because the visibility rule keeps it with the team and the instructors; keeping the main repository private during the course, rejected because the course's public model is a course-wide decision; putting the privatisation rule in both files, rejected because a rule with two owners drifts; linking back to the Week 6 credentials instead of restating them, rejected because the handoff must be self-contained.
- **Where it lands:** [Visibility Requirements](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#the-usability-data-repository), [Repository Requirements](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#repository-setup), and [Weekly Report Requirements](../../requirements/weekly-report-requirements.md#private-submission-wrapper).

## A9-DEC-017: The UAT-nn Family And The Customer's Two Runs

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the user acceptance tests are the `UAT-nn` family, two digits, maintained at `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md`, one end-to-end journey each; each section states the journey on the first line, then `**Status:**`, then the start state, the steps, the expected result, and a runs table with one row per run: sprint, date, the actual runner, build, result, evidence, and `DEC-nnn`; the evidence is the sanitized meeting report's anchor, and a run is cited as the test's section plus the sprint, such as `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md#uat-01` and `SPRINT-09`; the record has a `## The Tests` index.
  The team defines three initial tests, `UAT-01` to `UAT-03`.
  The customer runs them at the Sprint 9 review.
  At least three active UATs must pass during the handoff.
  Dropped tests do not count.
  If the customer cannot access the app, the team runs the tests live during the meeting for the customer to observe and judge.
  Record the actual runner as `Customer` or `Team`.
  Keep a dropped test, its identifier, its past runs, and the date and reason it was dropped.
  If a test needs a substantial change to its journey, start state, steps, or expected result, drop it and assign its replacement the next unused `UAT-nn`.
  Each review has one `DEC-nnn` for the UAT set, and the handoff verdict has its own `DEC-nnn`.
  The Week 9 and Week 10 reports link their session and keep each recorded `Fail`.
  The team records `Not run` with the reason when a test could not be checked.
  Use `<production-url>`, synthetic seed data, and a test account.
  The Moodle wrappers carry private access details.
  Keeping a test under ten minutes is recommended.
- **Why:** the customer gives the verdict on what is observed during the meeting.
  Run history shows which test was checked on which build, and a substantially revised test has its own identifier.
- **Alternatives considered:** a per-test decision would duplicate the set verdict.
  Reusing an identifier for a different test would make the original run misleading.
- **Where it lands:** `requirements/user-acceptance-tests-requirements.md`, [the plan](../plans/a9-plan.md#design-decisions), and [Customer Meetings Requirements](../../requirements/customer-meetings-requirements.md).
