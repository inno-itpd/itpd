# Assignment 6 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignment 6 and the requirements it depends on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignment and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A6-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the week's scope to the order its parts are worked in.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
An entry that reverses an `A5-DEC` records the whole reversal and restates the clauses that survive, because a reversal cannot be partial.
Mechanical edits — backlog housekeeping and the dates a decision here sets — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A6-DEC-001: Week 6 Is Product Analytics And Observability

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 6 is the whole of the product analytics and the observability: `docs/analytics.md` with its metric tree and events, the analytics backend and its two dashboards, `docs/observability.md` with its `SLI`s, the OpenObserve backend and its two dashboards, the two functional requirements and the two internal stories, the two architectural decision records and the container view, the observability quality requirement and its test, the optional runtime re-run that names an `SLI`, the threshold review, `REL-06`, the Sprint Review, the retrospective, and the AI usage report.
  The week has no pitch deck, it runs no experiment, it requires no log, trace, or alert, and it has no end users beyond the customer and the team's test traffic.
  Logs and traces are taught and are optional; an alert is a rule over a signal and is optional.
- **Why:** the lecture teaches metrics, product analytics, SLA/SLO/SLI, and observability, and the week's scope has to be one account rather than a syllabus row, a deck, and a set of rules that disagree.
  A week with no end users cannot run an experiment that would produce a result, so setting up the metrics and saying whether the focus metric has a value is the honest version of the work.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the 2025 Assignment 6's logs, traces, alerts, Grafana Cloud, and walkthrough recording, rejected because the current course port keeps OpenTelemetry, the `SLI`s, and the two self-hosted backends and grades the metrics and the dashboards; and designing only the analytics and leaving the observability, the stories, and the records to the syllabus, rejected because no file would own their shape.
- **Where it lands:** [Assignment 6](../../assignments/assignment-6.md), [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-6-oct-30--nov-5-product-analytics--observability), and [The Parts Assignment 6 Carries](../plans/a6-plan.md#the-parts-assignment-6-carries).

## A6-DEC-002: Two Maintained Plans, One Metric Tree, And One Event Registry

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/analytics.md` and `docs/observability.md` are the two maintained artifacts, and the `SLI` lives in `docs/observability.md`.
  The key metric's only name is **focus metric**, and the page has no separate value moment, so the focus metric traces to the `VP-nn` directly.
  The analytical metrics are `###` sections under `## The Value And The Metrics`, cited by their heading anchors as a feature is cited, and there is no `MET-nn` family.
  The metric tree is one nested list under `### The Metric Tree` and before the justification: one focus metric, one counter-metric nested at L1 under it and labelled as guarding it, one L1 input, and one or two L2 inputs; each node carries its level, `Focus`, `L1`, `L1 (counter-metric)`, or `L2`, and links its metric's section.
  Each metric and each `SLI` records its query, and the dashboard chart names the metric or `SLI` and runs it; the quality requirements matrix lists the requirements and not their `SLI`s.
  The event table is the one registry, with `Event`, `Fires when`, `Fields`, and `Collected at`, and there is no section per event and no `Metrics fed` column: an event is cited by its name plus `#the-events`.
  One naming convention, such as `object_action` in snake case, is stated above the table and applies to every row.
- **Why:** a metric and an event are joined facts rather than records with their own lifecycle, so an identifier family would create a second place to keep each of them current.
  One owner per fact is what keeps a chart's query and a page's query from drifting: the page carries the query and the chart runs it.
  The tree is the shortest form that says which metric sits under which, and indentation is what carries that relation.
  It is also the page's map, so it comes before the justification and every node is a link: a reader meets the numbers first, reads each node's level from its label, and jumps to the metric's own section.
  The counter-metric is nested at L1 rather than drawn beside the focus metric, because a sibling has no parent, reads as a second focus metric, and makes the tree's own one-parent rule false.
  It stays a guard rather than an input metric, so the dashboard's "its L1 input" names one metric and the L1 input keeps its meaning as a step the team can change.
  The naming convention sits above the table, so a reader meets it before the names it governs.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `MET-nn` family with a file per metric, rejected because a metric has no fields beyond its definition, source, and query and its section already holds them; a `Metrics fed` column on the event table, rejected because a metric's `**Source:**` already names its events and a reverse column would be a second copy that drifts; a value moment beside the focus metric, rejected because the course has no end users to have one and the focus metric traces to the value proposition directly; drawing the counter-metric as the focus metric's sibling, rejected because the tree would then have two roots and a node with no parent; a bare nested list of metric names with no level labels and no links, rejected because the tree then neither navigates the page nor says which node is the focus metric and which an input; and a justification before the tree, rejected because a reader meets the argument for a number before the number.
- **Where it lands:** [Product Analytics Requirements](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md), [Observability Requirements](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md), and [Step 1: Find The Metrics](../../guides/analytics-and-observability.md#step-1-find-the-metrics).

## A6-DEC-003: Two Self-Hosted Backends, One Per Environment

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the analytics data comes from OpenPanel by default and the observability data from OpenObserve by default, both self-hosted on the university VM, and another documented backend is allowed for either.
  Both are separated by environment: one environment scope per environment — an OpenPanel project with its own client ID, an OpenObserve organization, or the documented backend's equivalent.
  Both are reachable from where the customer works on the university network, and both are addressed in Moodle only.
  The event data is private and lives outside the repository.
- **Why:** both backends are open source and run on the team's own machine, which is the only infrastructure the course provides and the only place the data can stay inside the university network.
  The environment separation is what lets the team test a pipeline without writing the production metric: a single project or organization would mix the test traffic into the customer's numbers with no query that could clean it afterwards.
- **Alternatives considered:** a cloud backend such as Grafana Cloud or an analytics SaaS, rejected because the data would leave the university network and the addresses and credentials would be public; one project for both environments, rejected because a dry run then pollutes the production metric; and requiring the two backends on the VM with no exception, rejected because a small VM cannot always run both: when it cannot, one runs on a second machine on the university network, and the team declares the deviation.
- **Where it lands:** [The Pipeline](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md#the-pipeline), [The Pipeline](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-pipeline), and [Environments And Deployment](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#environments-and-deployment).

## A6-DEC-005: Week 6 Has The Week 5 Shape And Runs No Experiment

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the syllabus's Week 6 heading becomes `Product Analytics & Observability`, the focus row names the lecture, the analytics, the observability, the focus metric, the four dashboards, and Submission 6, the Class 6 line names the lecture's five sections, and the week gains objectives, numbered deliverables with their paths, a corrected Submission 6 row, and the assignment.
  The week's scope is the lecture's metrics, product analytics, SLA/SLO/SLI, and observability; the hypothesis section is background, and the week runs no experiment because the course has no end users beyond the customer and the team's test traffic.
  The focus metric is the metric the team would track if the system were operated, and at least one analytics metric is computed by its query.
  No week gains logs, traces, alerts, or an experiment.
- **Why:** the syllabus's old heading promised telemetry, event tracking, instrumentation, analytics architectures, and data-driven decisions, and the lecture teaches a different set, while the week had no objectives and three terse deliverables with no student-facing path.
  A hypothesis with no users cannot be tested, so an experiment would produce a claim rather than a finding; setting up the metrics and recording whether the focus metric has a value is what the week can honestly deliver.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the old objectives and deliverables, rejected because they name no artifact the week produces and the deck they carried has moved; and running the lecture's hypothesis as a graded experiment, rejected because the only traffic is the team's own and a result from it would be invented.
- **Where it lands:** [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-6-oct-30--nov-5-product-analytics--observability), [Assignment 6](../../assignments/assignment-6.md), and [Scope And Contradictions](../plans/a6-plan.md#scope-and-contradictions).

## A6-DEC-006: `SLI-nn` Is The Week 6 Family

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `SLI-nn` is repository-wide, takes two digits, and is headed `### SLI-01` under `## The SLIs`, with its name on the first line, so its anchor is `#sli-01`.
  Each `SLI` carries `**Status:**` `Active`, `Planned`, or `Dropped`, and a `**Dropped:**` field with the date and the reason when it is dropped.
  There is no `SLO-nn` family and no `MET-nn` family.
  A runtime test written from Week 6 on names an `Active` `SLI` in `**Runs at:**`, and a dropped `SLI` leaves its requirement active when another `Active` one covers it: the test names the replacement or its testing-plan row becomes `Not verified`.
  A test that names an `SLI` reads it, including a Week 5 test re-run with the citation.
- **Why:** a signal serves every requirement whose test reads it, so it is not scoped to one `QR-nn` as a `QRT-nn` is; the bare number is then unique across the repository and its heading is the citation.
  The heading is a `###` section under the section that introduces the `SLI`s, so the page's top-level headings stay the plan's parts and the `SLI`s read as one group.
  `Planned` and `Dropped` are needed because a team lists the `SLI`s its requirements need before it demonstrates one, and a signal it abandons must stay in the record.
  An `SLO` record would be a second target beside the requirement's `Response Measure`, and a `MET-nn` family would give an analytics metric a lifecycle it does not have.
- **Alternatives considered:** scoping `SLI-nn` to a `QR-nn`, rejected because two requirements may read one signal and the pair would then name the same record twice; a heading that repeats the name, rejected because the name is on the first line and the anchor type stays short; a top-level `## SLI-01` heading beside `## The SLIs`, rejected because the `SLI` sections would then sit beside the section that introduces them instead of under it; and an `SLO-nn` record per `SLI`, rejected because the requirement's measure already states the target.
- **Where it lands:** [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules), [The SLIs](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-slis), and [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks).

## A6-DEC-007: A Runtime Test Names An Active `SLI`, And Its Pass Criterion Settles Its Threshold

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a runtime `QRT-nn` written from Week 6 on, at level `scripted`, `deployed`, or `manual`, names the `Active` `SLI-nn` it reads in `**Runs at:**`.
  A `manual` test that reads no signal is exempt and says so in `**Why Manual:**`.
  Its `### Procedure` reads the signal, and any test that names an `SLI` in `**Runs at:**`, including a Week 5 test re-run, reads it too.
  Its `**Pass when:**` states the requirement's `Response Measure` made specific to the test's environment and window, and still settles the measure's threshold.
  A particular request or event may be the instance the procedure produces, but it does not narrow the threshold.
  A `CI` test cites no `SLI`, no separate `SLO-nn` record is written, and the test carries no `Samples` field.
- **Why:** Week 5 made the pass criterion restate the requirement's measure, and Week 6 adds a signal over that measure, so the criterion needs a place to name what it reads without turning one request into a new bar.
  The requirement's measure stays the target because it is the thing the customer accepted; a separate `SLO` would make the same target in a second place and go stale.
- **Alternatives considered:** letting the pass criterion name the one failed request as the threshold, rejected because a single request is an instance and the measure is about the requirement; an `SLO-nn` record per runtime test, rejected because the target already lives in the requirement; and a `Samples` field on the test, rejected because the procedure already states how many runs it makes.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [The Quality Requirement](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirement), and [The SLIs](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-slis).

## A6-DEC-008: The Observability Requirement Is Diagnosability In The Product

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the week adds one quality requirement, `Maintainability` / `Analysability`, about the product's diagnosability: an operator can find the request's point and the `SLI`'s five-minute P95 result for a failed request and see which request failed and what happened, in staging.
  The `SLI`'s value is the P95 over five minutes; the product's telemetry endpoint also exposes the per-request point with the request identifier, so the test can find the request.
  Its `Response Measure` is that the product's telemetry endpoint exposes that point within five seconds and it carries the request identifier and the failure labels, and the five-minute P95 query returns the failure series within one minute.
  It carries `**Business importance:** High` and `**Technical risk:** Medium`, its test is `deployed`, short-window, and `Passes` in `SPRINT-06`, and it is the week's required demonstration.
  The OpenObserve query and the dashboard are evidence that the pipeline stores and renders the signal, not the system under test.
  No analytics quality requirement is added: analytics is a product capability, and its stack choice is an ADR that addresses its functional requirement.
- **Why:** an operator who cannot find a failed request in the product's own signals cannot diagnose the product, and that is a property of the product rather than of the dashboard.
  The endpoint is the measured artifact because the product emits the signal; the backend is replaceable, and a requirement about a dashboard would grade a tool the team might swap.
  Analytics has no ISO/IEC 25010 quality of its own beyond being usable, so a second quality requirement would exist only to give the ADR an `**Addresses:**` target.
- **Alternatives considered:** requiring the dashboard to show the point, rejected because a dashboard that renders a series the product never emitted is not diagnosability; adding an analytics quality requirement, rejected because its ADR can address its functional requirement; and making the test a `manual` inspection, rejected because the point's five-second exposure is a check a command can settle.
- **Where it lands:** [Assignment 6](../../assignments/assignment-6.md), [The Quality Requirement](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirement), and [Step 6: Write The Observability Requirement And Run Its Test](../../guides/analytics-and-observability.md#step-6-write-the-observability-requirement-and-run-its-test).

## A6-DEC-009: Two ADRs, And Two Backends As External Systems

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the week adds two architectural decision records: the analytics record addresses the analytics functional requirement, and the observability record addresses the observability quality requirement and the observability functional requirement.
  Each `**Containers:**` names the product containers that emit the events or the signals.
  The two backends are external systems, the container set is unchanged, no new view is drawn, and the container view's arrow to each backend carries the protocol.
  The container view and the legend say, once each, that the team self-hosts both and does not build them; the vision's context diagram is unchanged.
- **Why:** each stack choice is a technology choice, and an architectural decision record has to address at least one requirement; the two records are what let the choices be cited from the requirements and the features.
  Both backends are outside the product boundary: the product sends telemetry to them, and collection is switched off on a developer's machine, so neither is needed for the product to work and neither is a container.
  The team deploys them, but a container is a runtime the product needs, not simply something the team runs; the container set is what the FRs and the ADRs' `**Containers:**` fields are checked against.
- **Alternatives considered:** one record for both backends, rejected because the two choices answer different requirements and one record would split its `**Addresses:**`; adding the backends as containers, rejected because the product does not need either to work; drawing them on the vision's context diagram, rejected because the context is the product's business environment and an operational backend is not a boundary job; and drawing a pipeline diagram, rejected because no rule requires one and the deployment table carries the structure.
- **Where it lands:** [The Record](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#the-record), [Container View](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#container-view), [System Context](../../requirements/product-vision-requirements.md#system-context), [Draw The Container View](../../guides/quality-and-architecture.md#draw-the-container-view), and [Part 7](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-7-record-the-two-adrs-and-update-the-container-view).

## A6-DEC-011: The Required Test Is Short-Window, And The Re-Run Is Optional

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the observability requirement's `QRT` is a short-window `deployed` check that `Passes` in `SPRINT-06`: it generates the core task's traffic in staging, fails one request, waits up to five seconds, reads the request's point from the product's telemetry endpoint, and passes when the endpoint exposes the point with the request identifier and the failure labels and the `SLI`'s five-minute P95 query returns the failure series within one minute.
  A testing-plan row planned for `SPRINT-06` that cannot run moves to `SPRINT-07` with a reason.
  Re-running an implemented runtime `QRT` with the same `Active` `SLI` is optional, its procedure reads that signal, and a failed re-run records `Fails` and does not remove the week's demonstration.
  A longer test a team runs on its own stays `Not verified` with its `Planned for sprint`, and no testing-plan status is added.
- **Why:** Week 5's plan left rows planned for `SPRINT-06`, and the week's deadline cannot wait for a window that is still open; a short-window check can pass before the deadline and still exercises the whole pipeline.
  The required demonstration is the observability requirement's test, so a failed re-run must not take it away, and a team with no suitable signal for a re-run is not blocked.
- **Alternatives considered:** making the re-run required, rejected because the week already has a required `SLI` demonstration and the re-run depends on a requirement the team may not have; and adding an `In progress` status for open windows, rejected because `Not verified` with its planned sprint already says it.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Testing Plan](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#quality-requirement-testing-plan), [Part 9](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-9-write-the-observability-requirement-and-its-test-and-run-the-test), and [Part 13](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-13-redeploy-and-retest-after-same-week-rework).

## A6-DEC-012: The Four Dashboards' Addresses Are Private-Only

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** all four dashboards' addresses and credentials, and the two backends' addresses and all credentials, including client IDs and tokens, are Moodle-only.
  The two plans, the tests' `**Runs at:**`, and the report use placeholders or environment variables such as `$ANALYTICS_STAGING_URL` and `$OPENOBSERVE_URL`.
  The dashboards are reached from the university network, the screenshots are sanitized, and no link is excluded from the link check.
- **Why:** the repository is public while the backends are reachable from the university network only, so a committed address would be a private value in a public file and would not help a reader outside the network.
  The address is evidence the customer needs, which is what the Moodle wrapper is for, and a link excluded from the check is how a private address stays in the repository.
- **Alternatives considered:** committing the addresses and excluding them from the link check, rejected because the file is public and the exclusion would only hide the leak; and putting the addresses in `.env.example`, rejected because the example carries the shape and not the value.
- **Where it lands:** [Sensitive Information Reference](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#sensitive-information-reference), [The Dashboard](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md#the-dashboard), and [Assignment Report On Moodle](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#assignment-report-on-moodle).

## A6-DEC-013: The Threshold Review Is Preparation

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the Week 6 threshold review is preparation for the Sprint Review and lives in the report's `## Threshold review` section as one table: one row per goal that is `Open` when the review starts, with the goal linked, its current measure, whether the analytics or the observability data can settle it, and any proposed measure.
  The team may propose an analytics-measured goal or a revised `**Measure:**`, and a proposed measure states its number, the week it lands in, and the metric's `###` link.
  Only the customer's `DEC-nnn` at the Sprint Review moves the bar, and the meeting report records it in `## Decisions`.
  A goal whose measure is already settled is `Achieved` or `Missed` with `**Outcome:**`, one whose measure lands later stays `Open`, and no goal is required to change.
  The report names each reviewed goal's status in a sentence after the table, links the goal to its section, and leaves a settled goal's `**Outcome:**` in `docs/threshold-of-success.md`; it says what was found and links the evidence, and the report does not restate it.
- **Why:** the threshold was written in Week 3 with no metrics, so Week 6 is the first week in which the team can say whether a goal is measurable; making the review preparatory keeps the bar with the customer, as the plan's rule already requires.
  Requiring a goal to change would turn a new capability into pressure to move a target the customer set.
  The table fixes the four facts a review states, so every team's review is read the same way, and the goal's own fields still own its maintained status and outcome.
- **Alternatives considered:** moving a goal in the plan with the team's own decision, rejected because a bar move needs the customer's agreement; requiring at least one analytics-measured goal, rejected because not every team has a metric that fits a goal by Week 6; and adding a `Threshold` section to the plan, rejected because the threshold file and the report already own the goal and the review.
- **Where it lands:** [The Threshold Of Success](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#the-threshold-of-success), [Part 10](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-10-review-the-threshold-of-success), and [Step 8: Review The Threshold](../../guides/analytics-and-observability.md#step-8-review-the-threshold).

## A6-DEC-014: No Event Or Signal Carries Personal Data

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** no event or signal carries personal data as `visibility-requirements.md` defines it: a name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, or free text that identifies a person.
  An event uses an opaque identifier and no free text, every event carries `source`, and `smoke` marks the team's own pipeline check.
  The analytics plan lists what is collected and what is never collected, the telemetry lives outside the repository, and a dashboard screenshot is sanitized before it is published.
- **Why:** telemetry payloads are the easiest private data to commit by accident, because they look like test fixtures and a screenshot of a dashboard can carry them; the repository is public and a file deleted after a commit stays in the history.
  An opaque identifier keeps per-user analysis possible without carrying a person's identity, which is the combination the week's metrics need.
- **Alternatives considered:** a hashed email address, rejected because a hash is the email address with one step in front of it and can be attacked; free-text fields with a note to sanitize them, rejected because nothing keeps such a note current; and committing the collected data as evidence, rejected because the evidence is a sanitized screenshot.
- **Where it lands:** [Telemetry And Personal Data](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#telemetry-and-personal-data), [The Events](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md#the-events), and [Collected Data](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#collected-data).

## A6-DEC-015: The Release Body Is The Upgrade Note, And The Plan Records The Checks

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `REL-06`'s release body is the upgrade note for whoever deploys it: it states whether anything beyond deploying this version is needed, names the new environment variables and configuration, and ends with the `[Full changelog](<absolute url>)` line.
  The technical record is the plan's `REL-06` block with `**Released:**` and `**Checked QRs:**`, and the analytics and the observability are `### Added` changelog entries, because they add configuration and a deployment.
- **Why:** the release page's reader is the person running the previous version, and a test list on that page duplicates what the testing plan already keeps current.
  `A5-DEC-042` settled the body's reader and the changelog's groups, and the two new pipelines are configuration and deployment changes an operator would have to act on, which is what the changelog records.
- **Alternatives considered:** putting the checked requirements in the body, rejected because the plan's block is the maintained record and the body would go stale; and recording the pipelines under a changelog group other than `### Added`, rejected because they add configuration and capability rather than change or remove one.
- **Where it lands:** [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release), [Releases](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#releases), and [Part 14](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-14-cut-the-rel-06-release).

## A6-DEC-026: The Diagnosability Test Reads The Staging Pipeline's Query

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Reverses:** [`A6-DEC-023`](#a6-dec-023-the-deployed-level-is-kept-as-week-5-defined-it), in full: its clause that the backend is evidence rather than the test's read path does not hold, and its endpoint clause does not hold; the clauses that survive are restated here, and the endpoint clauses of `A6-DEC-008` and `A6-DEC-011` are superseded.
- **Decision:** the product pushes a response-time histogram and a structured per-request log line to OpenObserve; it does not expose a separate telemetry endpoint.
  The diagnosability requirement's test reads the signal through the staging organization's query API: the log line by `request_id` within five seconds, and the `SLI`'s five-minute P95 query within one minute.
  The dashboard is evidence, and the chart is not the measurement.
  `scripted` and `deployed` keep their Week 5 meanings: the test is an automated check against the staging deployment, run from the university network, and `A5-DEC-003` stands.
  The 5s and 1min windows are met by configuring the log exporter's batch at about one second and the metric exporter's interval at about ten seconds; the deployment runbook records them, and the `SLI`'s `**Window:**` records the target.
  The structured log line is required for the core task because the demonstrated `SLI`'s per-request point is read from it; the trace remains optional.
- **Why:** a metrics-only push cannot produce one point per request with `request_id`, because a histogram is aggregate and a metric labelled with `request_id` would be one series per request; the old endpoint was an invented product interface that no requirement defined.
  Querying the staging organization measures the operator's time to find the signal end to end and keeps the dashboard as evidence, while the product's instrumentation remains the artifact and the staging environment remains the test bed.
  The distinction that matters is the signal versus the chart, not the product versus the backend: a test that reads a chart grades a tool, and a test that reads the stored signal measures diagnosability.
- **Alternatives considered:** a product read endpoint at `/telemetry/sli`, rejected because it adds a product surface the team does not otherwise need and the push is the chosen architecture; an aggregate-only requirement, rejected because it loses which request failed; reading the dashboard, rejected because a chart is not a measurement; and a new `deployed` exception, rejected because the test still runs against staging and only its read path changes.
- **Where it lands:** [The Signals](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-signals), [The Pipeline](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-pipeline), [The SLIs](../../requirements/observability-requirements.md#the-slis), [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Step 6](../../guides/analytics-and-observability.md#step-6-write-the-observability-requirement-and-run-its-test), [Part 9](../../assignments/assignment-6.md#part-9-write-the-observability-requirement-and-its-test-and-run-the-test), and [the plan's scope and contradictions](../plans/a6-plan.md#scope-and-contradictions).
