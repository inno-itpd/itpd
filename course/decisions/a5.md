# Assignment 5 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignment 5 and the requirements it depends on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignment and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A5-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the week's scope to the order its parts are worked in.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
An entry that reverses an `A3-DEC` or an `A4-DEC` records the whole reversal, so `A5-DEC-017` restates the clauses of `A3-DEC-018` that stay standing, because a reversal cannot be partial.
Mechanical edits — routing, backlog housekeeping, and the dates a decision here sets — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A5-DEC-001: Week 5 Is The Whole Week Outside The Pitch Deck

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 5 is the whole week outside the pitch deck: the quality requirement tests, the testing plan, the CI checks and the automated tests, the environments and the manual deployment, at least two plan milestones and two new stories, the codebase design exercise, `REL-05`, the Sprint Review, the retrospective, and the AI usage report.
  Week 5 has no pitch deck, and the in-class presentations are in the Week 7 class, where a near-final Week 6 deck is shown before its 7 November submission.
- **Why:** the plan began as a scope plan for three groups, and the week's other parts would otherwise have been owned by a syllabus row, a rule, and a plan that disagree; designing the whole week in one place is what lets every part name its owner.
  The deck is Week 6's deliverable, so a Week 5 deck would present the same weeks of work twice, and the Week 7 class shows a near-final deck after the analytics work it reports on.
- **Alternatives considered:** designing only the three groups and leaving the milestones, the stories, the codebase design exercise, the review, the release, the retrospective, and the AI usage report to the syllabus, rejected because no file would own their shape; and keeping a Week 5 deck, rejected because the deck is Week 6's deliverable and Week 5 has no repository artifact for it.
- **Where it lands:** [Assignment 5](../../assignments/assignment-5.md), [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-5-oct-23--oct-29-testing-verification--deployment), and [The Parts Assignment 5 Carries](../plans/a5-plan.md#the-parts-assignment-5-carries).

## A5-DEC-002: A Test Lives With The Requirement It Proves

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a quality requirement test is a `## QRT-nn` section inside its `QR-nn` file, after the requirement's `## Scenario`, numbered within the requirement from `QRT-01`, and `quality-requirements-requirements.md` owns the requirement and its tests together; no separate tests file is added.
- **Why:** the requirement and the check that proves it are read as one record, as a story and its acceptance criteria already are, so a test follows the shape the course uses.
  A separate file per test would make the pair a citation across two files, while a project-wide test number would claim a scope the test does not have.
- **Alternatives considered:** one tests file per quality requirement under a `tests/` directory, rejected because it splits a claim from its check and adds a path with no rule of its own; and a repository-wide `QRT-nn` sequence, rejected because a test belongs to its requirement and is cited as the pair.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Part 4](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-4-write-the-quality-requirement-tests), and [Write The Test](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#step-2-write-the-test).

## A5-DEC-003: The Test States Its Environment And The Plan Records Its Result

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a test's `**Level:**` is `CI`, `scripted`, `deployed`, or `manual`, `**Runs at:**` says what runs it, and a manual test carries `**Why Manual:**`; the test carries no check result, and the testing plan carries its result, its last check, its planned sprint, and its `Evidence` cell.
- **Why:** the level is the fact that decides where a check can run and what evidence proves it, so it belongs on the definition that does not change between runs.
  The result changes every sprint while the definition does not, so writing it on the test would rewrite an identifier's section at every check and leave the same fact in two places.
- **Alternatives considered:** carrying `**Checked:**`, the result, and `**Evidence:**` on the test, rejected because the definition would be rewritten on every run and would duplicate the plan; and a free-text level, rejected because the level is what selects the evidence and environment rules.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Quality Requirement Testing Plan](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#quality-requirement-testing-plan), [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-5-run-the-tests-and-record-the-testing-plan), and [Record The Progress](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#step-4-record-the-progress).

## A5-DEC-004: A High Requirement Gets An Executable Test, And A Manual One Is The Documented Exception

- **Status:** Accepted, with its release-claim clause reversed by [A5-DEC-038](#a5-dec-038-the-release-body-is-user-facing-and-the-plan-records-the-checks); the rest of the entry stands.
- **Decision:** every active `High` business-importance quality requirement has a `CI`, `scripted`, or `deployed` test; when no automated form is possible, a `manual` test is the documented exception for that requirement, carrying `**Why Manual:**` and a declared deviation naming the requirement, and the release may claim the requirement through a passing test of any level.
- **Why:** a `High` business-importance requirement is the customer's own priority, so a command must settle it rather than a person's impression; some attributes, such as usability, have no command that can run, so a rule with no exception would either be unwritten or quietly ignored.
  The declared deviation is what keeps the exception visible to the customer instead of turning into a silent lower bar.
- **Alternatives considered:** requiring an automated test with no exception, rejected because a legitimate requirement would then be untestable and the team would either break the rule or misuse a level; and accepting a manual test with no reason and no deviation, rejected because every requirement could then be reduced to an inspection.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release), [Part 4](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-4-write-the-quality-requirement-tests), and [Justify A Manual Test](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#justify-a-manual-test).

## A5-DEC-005: The Testing Plan Is One Table In The Project Plan

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `docs/project-plan.md`'s `## Quality Requirement Testing Plan`, after `## Releases` and before `## Progress Monitoring`, holds one row per test, in the order of the quality requirements index, and `planning-requirements.md` owns its columns and its rules; a `Fails` row keeps its `Checked` and `Evidence`, a `Not verified` row keeps its planned sprint, and a `Dropped` row has `None` for `Checked`, `Planned for sprint`, and `Evidence`.
  The quality requirements index's link moves from its `**Since: W4**` rule to a `**Since: W5**` rule rather than gaining a second one.
- **Why:** the project plan already says what the project is doing and when, and a test's result is progress of the same kind, so the table sits where a reader looks for what remains and `## Progress Monitoring` reads it in the same file.
  The index is the priority matrix and keeps records rather than results, so it links the plan once; moving the existing link rather than adding a rule keeps one owner.
- **Alternatives considered:** the rows in the weekly report, rejected because the report is a dated record and the current result would then sit in the week it was checked; a spreadsheet outside the repository, rejected because the plan is the maintained record and a second system would drift; and keeping the link rule in Week 4, rejected because the section does not exist until Week 5.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Testing Plan](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#quality-requirement-testing-plan), [The Quality Requirements Index](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirements-index), [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-5-run-the-tests-and-record-the-testing-plan), and [Record The Progress](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#step-4-record-the-progress).

## A5-DEC-006: A Test Inherits Its Requirement's Priority

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** a test is not prioritized separately: it inherits the priority of the quality requirement it proves, the testing plan's rows follow the order of the quality requirements index, and `Planned for sprint` is a test's only schedule.
- **Why:** the matrix already ranks the requirements, and a test's importance is the importance of what it proves; a second priority could disagree with the matrix and would need its own sort.
  A test that carried a date of its own would duplicate the plan's timeboxing, so the planned sprint is the only schedule.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `Priority` column in the testing plan, rejected because it duplicates the matrix and can drift from it; and a planned date on each test, rejected because the sprint is the plan's unit of time.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Testing Plan](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#quality-requirement-testing-plan), [The Quality Requirements Index](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirements-index), [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-5-run-the-tests-and-record-the-testing-plan), and [Give Every Test One Row](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#give-every-test-one-row).

## A5-DEC-007: The Evidence Follows The Level The Test Runs At

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the evidence a test produces follows its level: a `CI` test links its run on `main`, a `scripted` test records the command and its output, a `deployed` test records the command or tool, the staging environment, the output, the measured value, and the commit it ran against, with a screenshot when the result is visual, and a `manual` test records what was inspected, by whom, and what was observed.
  No evidence names a real address.
- **Why:** each level produces a different artefact, and the testing plan's `Evidence` cell has to point at something real; saying what each level must produce is what makes a `deployed` test different from a screenshot of the home page.
  The commit and the measured value are what let a reader tell the released state from a local run.
- **Alternatives considered:** one generic "link the evidence" rule, rejected because the level's obligation would be unstated and any link would satisfy a deployed test; and pasting the output into the `Evidence` cell, rejected because the plan would then hold the evidence and the dated report's copy would go stale.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Quality Requirement Testing Plan](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#quality-requirement-testing-plan), [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-5-run-the-tests-and-record-the-testing-plan), and [Link The Evidence Its Level Requires](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#link-the-evidence-its-level-requires).

## A5-DEC-008: The Release Claims Only Quality Its Tests Verified

- **Status:** Reversed by [A5-DEC-038](#a5-dec-038-the-release-body-is-user-facing-and-the-plan-records-the-checks)
- **Decision:** the release body links each test the release adds or relies on, and claims a `QR-nn` only when the requirement has at least one non-dropped test and every non-dropped test is `Passes` and `Checked` in the release's sprint, with evidence from the released state; a test last checked in an earlier sprint is checked again on the released commit first, and the body's shape is the team's.
- **Why:** the release is the moment a quality claim becomes public, and a requirement verified weeks ago says nothing about the tagged commit; requiring the check in the release's sprint is what ties the claim to the code that is released.
  The body is the right carrier because the changelog is user-facing and a test is not a user-visible change.
- **Alternatives considered:** claiming a quality requirement because it was verified once, rejected because the claim would then outlive the commit that was verified; and recording the tests in the changelog, rejected because Keep a Changelog's groups carry user-visible changes and a test is not one.
- **Where it lands:** [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release), [The Changelog](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#the-changelog), [Part 10](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-10-cut-the-rel-05-release), and [Common Mistakes](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#common-mistakes).

## A5-DEC-009: The Container View Is The Last Required View

  A team may draw a further view for itself, but no rule asks for it and no artifact cites it.
- **Why:** the container view's own rule already calls it the last view the architecture document gains, and no Week 5 rule, part, or guide step ever consumed a component or sequence view; a view no rule requires and no artifact cites is work with no reader.
  Recording the reversal rather than deleting the clauses keeps the decision logs honest about what changed and why.
- **Alternatives considered:** requiring the two views in Week 5 as the two earlier entries said, rejected because nothing in the week uses them and the architecture document's last required view would then not be last; and leaving the clauses as a deferral without an entry, rejected because a reversal needs its own record and a decision cannot be partially reversed.

## A5-DEC-010: The Container View Is The Only View That Names Containers

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the container view is the only view that lists containers, so the `**Containers:**` fields on the functional requirements and the architectural decision records are checked against the set it lists, and no later view can introduce a container the view does not show.
- **Why:** the two fields name units a reader must be able to look up, and the container view is the artifact that defines them; a second view drawing containers would give the fields a second set to be checked against.
  No week adds a further view, which is what keeps the set closed.
- **Alternatives considered:** letting a component view refine the set, rejected because the fields would then point at a view no rule required and the check would need two sources; and checking the fields against the record that decides the stack, rejected because the record settles the split while the view is what the reader consults.
- **Where it lands:** [Container View](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#container-view), [Container Responsibilities](../../requirements/architecture-requirements.md#container-responsibilities), [The Functional Requirement](../../requirements/functional-requirements-requirements.md#the-functional-requirement), and [Name The Containers](../../guides/quality-and-architecture.md#name-the-containers).

## A5-DEC-012: Continuous Integration Arrives In Week 5 And Deploys Nothing

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** continuous integration is `actionlint` over `.github/workflows/` plus every applicable product-code check among linting, formatting, type checking, build, and automated tests, on pull requests and on `main`, each pinned, and `actionlint` and the checks join the Definition of Done's pull request list; the workflow stops at the build and the tests, with no deployment job.
- **Why:** the week's lecture teaches testing and automation, so the week is where the checks the course requires become an obligation; a check a team is asked for before it has been taught is a rule with no lesson behind it.
  The product CI runs outside the university network, so it cannot reach the VM and a deployment job could only fail; the deployment is manual from a campus machine.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the checks in Week 4 as the earlier requirement set it, rejected once the teaching week was settled; and adding a deployment job, rejected because the product CI cannot reach the university network and the deployment is the week's manual gate.
- **Where it lands:** [Continuous Integration](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#continuous-integration), [The Pull Request](../../requirements/definition-of-done-requirements.md#the-pull-request), and [Part 2](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-2-add-the-automated-tests-and-the-ci-checks).

## A5-DEC-014: QRT-nn Is The Week 5 Family And Is Scoped To Its Requirement

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `QRT-nn` is the Week 5 identifier family, scoped to its `QR-nn`: it takes two digits, its identifier is the pair `QR-01 QRT-02`, its heading is `## QRT-nn` and its anchor `#qrt-nn` because the family is an exception to rule 5 and the requirement's file supplies the scope, so the bare number is unique only within its requirement; it carries `**Status:**` `Active` or `Dropped` and follows rules 10 and 11, so a dropped test keeps its first line and fields and adds `**Dropped:**` with the date and reason, and its testing-plan row says `Dropped` with no evidence.
  Every active `High` business-importance requirement has at least one test, a `Medium` or `Low` requirement may have one, a dropped requirement needs none, and the full example shows one test for each of its two active `High` business-importance requirements.
- **Why:** the test's heading has to be reachable from a repository-relative link, and the pair is what makes the number unambiguous once two requirements each have a `QRT-01`; a project-wide number would have to be allocated across branches for a record that never leaves its requirement.
  The status field and the dropped form are what make a test an identifier as the other families are, rather than a paragraph inside a requirement.
- **Alternatives considered:** a three-digit or project-wide `QRT-nnn`, rejected because the test's scope is its requirement and the pair already carries the scope; and a heading that repeats the requirement, rejected because the file already says which requirement it holds and the bare number keeps the anchor short.
- **Where it lands:** [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules), [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [Traceability Into Later Weeks](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#traceability-into-later-weeks), [Part 4](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-4-write-the-quality-requirement-tests), and [Write The Test](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#step-2-write-the-test).

## A5-DEC-018: An Architectural Decision Record Cites No Test

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** an architectural decision record cites no `QRT-nn`: its `**Addresses:**` list names the requirements the decision accounts for, so it names the `QR-nn` a test proves and reaches the test through it.
- **Why:** a test proves a requirement rather than a decision, and an `**Addresses:**` list already means the requirements the decision is responsible for; adding a test would make the list mean two things.
  The requirement is the stable end of the link, while a test can be dropped or replaced without the decision changing.
- **Alternatives considered:** adding the `QRT-nn` to `**Addresses:**`, rejected because the field's rule names `FR-nnn` and `QR-nn` and a test is neither; and a second field on the record for its tests, rejected because it would duplicate the testing plan and go stale.
- **Where it lands:** [The Record](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#the-record), [What It Accounts For](../../requirements/adr-requirements.md#what-it-accounts-for), and [Common Mistakes](../../guides/quality-requirement-tests.md#common-mistakes).

## A5-DEC-019: The Milestone Minimum Is The Assignment's, Not A Rule

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Assignment 5 asks for at least two plan milestones due in `SPRINT-05` through `SPRINT-09` in total; an existing milestone in that range counts and only the shortfall is added; the two are due in different sprints; a run may cover two sprints and may begin at `SPRINT-04` and end at `SPRINT-05`; the team chooses the due weeks, `planning-requirements.md` rule 6 keeps the milestone set the team's choice, and no rule is added.
- **Why:** the rule already leaves the set to the team, and a fixed `MLS-05` over `SPRINT-04` and `SPRINT-05` could collide with a team's existing milestone and would make a sprint belong to two milestones; a week-specific bar leaves the plan intact.
  Counting an existing milestone means a team that already planned the range is not asked to invent a second one.
- **Alternatives considered:** a fixed fifth-week milestone, rejected because it can conflict with an existing one and would turn the team's choice into a rule; and a rule in `planning-requirements.md`, rejected because the minimum is this week's bar and not an obligation for every week.
- **Where it lands:** [Milestones](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#milestones), [Part 1](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-1-plan-the-sprint-define-the-milestones-and-add-the-stories), and [Step 2: Choose The Roadmap's Zoom Level](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#step-2-choose-the-roadmaps-zoom-level).

## A5-DEC-020: Two Stories Carry The Next Two Increments

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the assignment asks for at least two new `US-nn`: at least one for the `SPRINT-05` increment, scheduled and shown at the Sprint Review, and one refined for `SPRINT-06`, with no milestone; each uses the existing Week 2 and Week 4 story form, so no requirements rule changes and the count is the week's minimum.

## A5-DEC-038: The Release Body Is User-Facing And The Plan Records The Checks

- **Status:** Accepted, with its body-shape and headings clauses extended by [A5-DEC-039](#a5-dec-039-the-release-body-may-carry-additional-content-after-the-note), so content may follow the `Full changelog` line; the rest of the entry stands.
- **Reverses:** [`A5-DEC-037`](#a5-dec-037-the-release-body-is-an-index-not-a-second-record), in full: its clauses that the body names the sprint the claim rests on, lists each claimed `QR-nn` with its tests, links the testing plan and the week's report, and records the production verification do not hold; its customer summary, limitation, no-metadata, and free-heading clauses are restated here.
  [`A5-DEC-027`](#a5-dec-027-the-release-body-carries-the-technical-verification), its clause that the release body carries the technical verification: its clause that the changelog stays the user-facing record stands, and its clause that the body's shape is the team's now covers headings only, because the three parts and their order are fixed; [A5-DEC-039](#a5-dec-039-the-release-body-may-carry-additional-content-after-the-note) extends it to the additional content after the `Full changelog` line.
  [`A5-DEC-008`](#a5-dec-008-the-release-claims-only-quality-its-tests-verified), in full: its claim condition, its body-as-carrier clause, and its recheck-before-claim rule do not hold, and the release makes no quality claim.
  [`A5-DEC-004`](#a5-dec-004-a-high-requirement-gets-an-executable-test-and-a-manual-one-is-the-documented-exception), its clause that the release may claim the requirement through a passing test of any level.
  [`A5-DEC-025`](#a5-dec-025-a-late-vm-postpones-the-deployment-and-changes-the-other-tests-level), its clauses that `REL-05` may claim the requirement through the fallback test and that the fallback claims only what a test verified.
- **Decision:** the release body is the customer-facing note for every release cut from Week 5 on: one paragraph on what the release lets the user do, or `No changes visible to a user`; one on what it does not do yet, or `None`; and a final `[Full changelog](...)` line to the changelog's released section.
  The body carries no `REL-nn`, `QR-nn`, or `QRT-nn` citation, claim, test result, testing-plan or week-report link, or production check.
  It restates no title, tag, commit, or date in its prose; the `Full changelog` URL's heading anchor may carry the version and the date.
  The team may add headings; the three parts and their order are fixed.
  The technical record is the sprint's `REL-nn` block in `docs/project-plan.md`: `**Released:**` links the tag and gives the day it was cut, and `**Checked QRs:**` lists every `QR-nn` with at least one test checked in the sprint, each linked to its file, with one link to the testing plan.
  The block is an index, not a claim: the testing plan's rows carry each test's result and show whether a requirement is fully or partially verified.
  The claim clauses of `A5-DEC-004` and `A5-DEC-025` are reversed the same way: a requirement appears in `Checked QRs` when a test was checked, and its row carries the result.
  Only the sprint's `REL-nn` carries the block; an earlier release in the same sprint and a hotfix, a release cut after it to repair what it released, carry none.
  The production core-task verification is deferred until the UAT work; until then the week report records it, per [`A5-DEC-025`](#a5-dec-025-a-late-vm-postpones-the-deployment-and-changes-the-other-tests-level), and `TODO.md` carries the deferral and the future `Checked UATs` field.
  `A5-DEC-032`'s cut after the review and the rework, and its tag on the final production deploy's commit, stand; its sentence that this makes the release body's claim true is historical.
- **Why:** the old body duplicated the testing plan's status, the `QRT-nn` sections, and the week's report on a public page, and the customer who opened it got a technical index instead of a note about the product.
  The plan already owns the sprint, so its `REL-nn` section is the maintained record of what the release checked, and the testing plan is the one place a result lives; a list of requirements is the smallest index that reaches their files and the rows.
  The claim condition made the release the author of a quality verdict that the plan already states test by test, and it hid the partials the customer and the grader need to see.
  The production verification has no field until the UAT work defines one, so the week report keeps the record honest in the meantime.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the claim in the body as a shorter index, rejected because the body would still carry the technical record and the plan's rows would still be the source of truth; listing only fully verified requirements in `Checked QRs` (the claim model), rejected because a requirement with one test checked and another still `Not verified` is exactly what the plan should show, and the block would then repeat the full/partial distinction; a separate `docs/releases.md` record, rejected because the plan already has the sprint section and a second file would be a second place to keep current; and adding the production field now, rejected because the UAT work has not defined what it would hold.

## A5-DEC-039: The Release Body May Carry Additional Content After The Note

- **Status:** Accepted, extending the body-shape and headings clauses of [A5-DEC-038](#a5-dec-038-the-release-body-is-user-facing-and-the-plan-records-the-checks)
- **Decision:** the body carries the required note — the summary or `No changes visible to a user`, the limitation or `None`, and the `Full changelog` line that closes it — and may carry additional content after that line, per [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release).
  The additional content is optional, the team chooses its headings, and everything in it is something a customer needs that no other record carries, such as an upgrade step, a screenshot, or where to find the change in the product.
  The whole-body prohibitions apply to it: no `REL-nn`, `QR-nn`, or `QRT-nn` citation, no quality claim, no test result, no testing-plan or week-report link, no production check, no metadata restatement, and no repeated changelog entry.
  The three parts and their order stay fixed.
- **Why:** the body has no technical job since `A5-DEC-038` moved the record to the plan's `REL-nn` block, so its only job is communication, and an upgrade step or a usage note is customer-facing guidance that the changelog does not carry and the limitation paragraph cannot say without turning into something it is not.
  Without the additional content, that guidance is either crammed into the limitation sentence or dropped.
  The ownership test is `A5-DEC-037`'s, whose content list the reversal retired while the test itself still holds.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping the body closed and clarifying that headings only organize the three parts, rejected because a team with a genuine upgrade step then has nowhere to put it; a named allowlist of an upgrade note and one image, rejected because the rule would become a menu rather than an ownership test, and an image is not the only extra a team needs; and allowing the content anywhere in the body, rejected because the customer's first blocks would vary and grading could no longer check a fixed opening.
- **Where it lands:** [Cutting A Release](../../requirements/releases-requirements.md#cutting-a-release), [Write The Release Body](../../guides/planning-and-tracking.md#write-the-release-body), and [Part 10](../../assignments/assignment-5.md#part-10-cut-the-rel-05-release).
