# Assignment 7 Design Decisions

This file records the key decisions behind Assignment 7 and the requirements it depends on, and the reason for each.
It is maintainer documentation for the course materials, not part of them: students follow the assignment and the requirements, and this file explains why those read the way they do.
The entries are `A7-DEC-nnn`, zero-padded and never renumbered or reused, in design order, from the week's scope to the order its parts are worked in.
A revision an entry received while the assignment was being written is folded into it, so each entry states the design as it now stands; a later reversal gets a new entry whose `**Reverses:**` names the one it replaces, as a `DEC-nnn` is reversed per [Reversing A Decision](../../requirements/decisions-requirements.md#reversing-a-decision).
An entry that reverses an `A6-DEC` records the whole reversal and restates the clauses that survive, because a reversal cannot be partial.
Mechanical edits — backlog housekeeping and the dates a decision here sets — are not design decisions and are not recorded here.

## A7-DEC-001: Week 7 Designs And The Team Schedules The Sessions

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 7 designs and prepares the usability test, and the team schedules the sessions when it can arrange representative participants, including before the design week's deadline.
  Week 7 writes `UT-01`, its record, its surveys, its tasks, its protocol, its consent, its private text repository, its recordings folder, its analysis plan, and its quality requirement with its manual test, and it recruits and schedules at least three participants with five as the target.
  Week 8 writes the sanitized notes and the results, records the `QR/QRT` verdict, cuts `REL-08`, and reports the results with Submission 8; the sessions may have run before it.
  Week 9 reflects on the results and prepares the 7-minute deck; it carries no usability results report.
  The Week 7 report claims no result.
- **Why:** the design is the week's deliverable, and a team that can arrange representative people earlier should not be held to a date; the results are Submission 8's, so the analysis and the learning land together in the week that reads them.
  The design is written before the first session, and the Week 7 report remains a design report.
- **Alternatives considered:** requiring the sessions to run after the design week's deadline, rejected because it turns a scheduling convenience into a rule and a team that can run earlier would sit on a finished design; running the analysis in Week 7, rejected because the results are Submission 8's and the Week 7 report would claim a result it does not yet own; keeping the whole test in Week 9, rejected because the week is the deck and reflection and the results would be written a month after the design; and writing the design in Week 7 and the results in Week 9, rejected because the Week 9 report would duplicate what the run week already found.
- **Where it lands:** [Assignment 7](../../assignments/assignment-7.md), [the syllabus](../syllabus.md#week-7-nov-6--nov-12-usability-testing-preparation), [The Week 8 Execution](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-week-8-execution), and [The Parts Assignment 7 Carries](../plans/a7-plan.md#the-parts-assignment-7-carries).

## A7-DEC-002: Week 7 Keeps The Full Sprint

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 7 keeps the full sprint: planning and refinement, `SPRINT-07`'s goal, due date, backlog link, and planned velocity, the design tasks scheduled in it, the sessions in the sprint they run in, `SPRINT-08` by default and `SPRINT-07` when they run before the design week's deadline, the results tasks in `SPRINT-08`, `REL-07`, the Sprint Review, the retrospective, and the AI usage report.
  The design tasks are scheduled in `SPRINT-07`, the sessions in the sprint they run in, and the results tasks in `SPRINT-08`, so each sprint's milestone lists the work it can finish.
  `**Planned Velocity:**` is `0` when the sprint carries only tasks.
- **Why:** every sprint from Week 3 to Week 9 has a release and every sprint from `SPRINT-04` has a retrospective, and a usability week is no exception; the test design is the sprint's increment even when it adds no product code.
  Scheduling the execution tasks in the earlier sprint would put work in a sprint that cannot finish it and would make the run week's plan a surprise.
- **Alternatives considered:** treating Week 7 as a documentation week with no release, rejected because the release rule is per sprint and a missing release breaks the weekly cadence; leaving the execution tasks unscheduled until Week 8, rejected because the Week 7 handoff then promises work no milestone carries.
- **Where it lands:** [Sprints](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#sprints), [Releases](../../requirements/planning-requirements.md#releases), and [Part 1](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-1-refine-the-product-backlog-and-plan-the-sprint).

## A7-DEC-003: `UT-nn` Is The Week 7 Family

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** `UT-nn` is the Week 7 identifier family, two digits, repository-wide.
  One test is one directory at `docs/usability-tests/ut-nn/`, its record is that directory's `README.md`, and the record's heading is `# UT-01: <title>`.
  The group's index is `docs/usability-tests/README.md`, with one row per test, and the record carries `**Status:**` `Planned`, `Completed`, or `Dropped` with a `**Dropped:**` field when dropped.
  A participant is `P-01`, `P-02`, and so on: two digits, local to its `UT-nn`, assigned in the order the sessions are scheduled, cited as the pair `UT-01 P-01`, and never reused.
  A finding is not a family: it is cited by `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/results.md#finding-1`.
- **Why:** a test is a record with a lifecycle, several participants, and a result, so it needs an identifier and a stable path, while a participant and a finding are scoped to one test and never cited outside it.
  The directory is lowercase because student paths are kebab-case and the identifier is uppercase, so the path and the identifier are different strings; the record's heading carries the identifier, and the `FR-nnn` and `SLI-nn` conventions are the precedent.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `P-nn` family for participants, rejected because a participant is meaningful only inside one test and a repository-wide code would be reused by the next test; a `FINDING-nn` family, rejected because a finding is a heading in one results file and an anchor is stable; and a file per test at `docs/usability-tests/UT-01.md`, rejected because a test needs a sessions directory and the images beside its record.
- **Where it lands:** [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules), [Where The Usability Tests Live](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#where-the-usability-tests-live), and [The Test Record](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-test-record).

## A7-DEC-004: The Private Usability-Data Repository And The Recordings Folder

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the team creates one private `<organization>/<repo>-usability-data` repository, private with the team and the instructors as readers, and it holds text only: the consent records, the identity mapping, the raw notes, the filtered telemetry export, the working analysis, and the recordings folder link and access.
  The recordings live in a team-owned cloud folder shared with the instructors, never in the product repository and not in the private repository.
  The public product repository links the private repository, and the link is excluded from the link check because it needs authentication, with the reason in the link check's configuration.
  The Moodle PDF carries both links and their access.
  The private repository, its history, and the recordings are deleted after the course is graded.
  When an instructor cannot access GitHub, the team gives the private text data through a Moodle-only archive and declares the deviation.
- **Why:** a recording is large and private and does not belong in a git history, while the consent records and the raw notes are small, editable, and reviewable, which is what a repository is good at.
  A private repository keeps the raw evidence one link away from the public record without making the public repository carry it, and the deletion rule is what makes the consent honest.
- **Alternatives considered:** putting the recordings in the private repository, rejected because a video is a large binary and the repository history would keep it forever; keeping the raw data only in the Moodle PDF, rejected because the PDF is a wrapper for the instructors and not a working store; committing the raw notes with a name check, rejected because a name is in the history the first time the check is missed.
- **Where it lands:** [The Usability Data Repository](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#the-usability-data-repository), [The Usability Data Repository](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#the-usability-data-repository), and [The Private Data Repository](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-private-data-repository).

## A7-DEC-005: The Sanitized Public Record And The Private Split

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the public product repository holds the sanitized test record, the surveys, the consent template, the session template, the sanitized session notes, the results, the findings, and the sanitized images, and it links the private repository.
  The visibility table's usability row changes from Moodle only to the public/private split, `course/rules.md`'s private-data sentence changes with it, and `general-requirements.md`'s private-only rule allows a private repository the instructors can access.
- **Why:** the weekly report has to show sanitized findings, and a result that lives nowhere public cannot be cited by the report, the reflection, or the deck; the identity and the raw words are what must stay private, not the finding.
  The old rule sent every result to Moodle, which is the same as having no result in the repository the course grades.
- **Alternatives considered:** keeping every result on Moodle, rejected because the Week 8 report and the Week 9 reflection cannot quote a result a reader cannot open; publishing the anonymized notes and the results but not the record, rejected because a result with no design behind it cannot be interpreted; and publishing the raw notes sanitized by hand, rejected because the identity mapping is exactly what may not be public.
- **Where it lands:** [Sensitive Information Reference](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#sensitive-information-reference), [The Rule](../rules.md#the-short-version), and [The Public Evidence](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-public-evidence).

## A7-DEC-006: At Least Three Representative Participants

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** at least three completed usability sessions with representative participants, and five as the design target.
  A participant is 18 or older and is not a teammate, the customer, the instructor, or the course staff; every counted participant matches the participant profile the record carries.
  A pilot and a withdrawn, unrecorded, unconsenting, or technically broken session do not count, and a participant who does not complete the task still counts.
  A replacement is recruited under the next free code, and the team recruits and schedules the sessions in Week 7; the sessions may run as soon as the design is written, and the results are due with Submission 8.
- **Why:** a usability test on the team measures the team, and a test on one outside person measures one person; three is the smallest set with a completion rate worth reading, and five is the design target so one cancellation does not drop below the minimum.
  The profile is what lets a reviewer decide whether a session counted, and the failure rule keeps a participant who struggles from being quietly replaced by a success.
- **Alternatives considered:** a hard five, rejected because recruiting five representative people is a target rather than a bar and a team with three counts; allowing a teammate as a fourth participant, rejected because the team's own knowledge makes the task unrepresentative; and counting a participant who withdrew, rejected because the recording and the data are incomplete.
- **Where it lands:** [The Participants](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-participants), [The Participant Profile](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-participant-profile), and [Part 6](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-6-plan-the-recruitment-and-the-sessions).

## A7-DEC-007: A Durable Consent And A Required Recording

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** every counted session has a durable consent recorded before it: a signed form, an email, a Telegram message, or another record the team can show the instructors.
  The consent covers the 18-or-older declaration, participation, the recording, the sanitized public results, the anonymized quotes, the private repository, the recordings folder, the instructors' access, the tools used and the deletion of their copies, the right to withdraw, and the deletion after grading.
  Consent to the recording and to the sanitized public results is required for a session to count; consent to an anonymized quote is separate and optional.
  A session without the required consent or the recording does not count.
- **Why:** the consent is the only thing that turns a person's words and screen into evidence the team may keep and publish, and the two required clauses are the two the record depends on: without the recording there is no observation, and without the public-results consent the finding cannot be cited.
- **Alternatives considered:** a verbal consent, rejected because the team cannot show it to an instructor; requiring the quote consent as well, rejected because it would exclude participants who are happy to be recorded but not quoted; and a single consent for everything, rejected because the quote is the one use a participant may reasonably refuse while still taking part.
- **Where it lands:** [Consent And Recording](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#consent-and-recording), [The Usability Data Repository](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#the-usability-data-repository), and [Part 4](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-4-write-the-consent-and-create-the-private-repository).

## A7-DEC-008: One Main And Two Secondary Usability Test Tasks

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the test has one main usability test task and two secondary usability test tasks.
  The main task is the product's main flow that the Week 6 event table covers and the quality requirement's subject, and the materials call it a usability test task and never `core task`.
  The tasks are scenario-based, identical for every participant, in a fixed order, with a start point, an end state, a success criterion written before the first session, a time limit, and a help rule, and their wording names no button, label, screen, step, or step count.
  The success criterion is `Unaided`, `Assisted`, or `Failed`.
  A dry run with a teammate checks the wording and the time limit, is not evidence, and is not counted.
- **Why:** one task would give one completion rate and no comparison, and a task menu would give the participant a different test from the next participant; the main task ties the session to the funnel and to the requirement, and the two secondary tasks show where the product's adjacent flows break.
  Naming a control would test the wording rather than the product, and a criterion written after the session grades the participant.
- **Alternatives considered:** a free exploration of the product, rejected because it produces no comparable outcome; three tasks of equal weight, rejected because the quality requirement and the funnel read one of them; and a task written with the button names, rejected because the participant then follows instructions and the friction it would find is the instructions'.
- **Where it lands:** [The Tasks](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-tasks), [The Protocol](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-protocol), and [Part 3](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-3-write-the-surveys-and-the-task-protocol).

## A7-DEC-009: The Instruments And The Optional Tools

- **Status:** Accepted, except its measure clause, which [A7-DEC-029](#a7-dec-029-the-team-chooses-the-measure) replaces
- **Decision:** every session records the pre-test background, the think-aloud observation, the task completion and assistance, the time on task, the SEQ after each task, the SUS after all the tasks, the SUS score, and the open-ended post-test.
  The pre-test asks for the role and background, the familiarity, the device, and the expectations, with no identifying field and with the 18-or-older declaration.
  The SEQ is one question after each task on a seven-point scale; the SUS is the standard ten items on the five-point scale, scored `SUS = 2.5 × (20 + sum(odd) - sum(even))`, and the results report its mean and median with the `n=<count>` caveat.
  The quality requirement reads one of the recorded measures, per [A7-DEC-029](#a7-dec-029-the-team-chooses-the-measure); the example reads the main task's median SEQ, and the results report the per-task SEQ.
  The qualitative evidence is the observation, the quotes, the friction, and the think-aloud; the quantitative evidence is the completion, the assistance, the time, the SEQ, the SUS, and the Week 6 events where they cover the task.
  The ASQ is optional.
- **Why:** the lecture's frameworks are the SEQ and the SUS, and a team that scores the SUS from a raw sum or leaves the SEQ out produces a number that is not comparable with anything; the pre-test and the post-test are the two surveys the lecture requires, and the observation is what says why a number is what it is.
  Two measures of ease, the SEQ and the SUS, cover a per-task and a whole-session reading without a third questionnaire.
- **Alternatives considered:** the ASQ instead of the SEQ, rejected because the ASQ needs three items and the SEQ's one question is the post-task measure the lecture teaches; the SUS alone, rejected because it reads the whole session and the quality requirement reads the main task; and a satisfaction score of the team's own, rejected because it is comparable with nothing.
- **Where it lands:** [The Instruments](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-instruments), [The Analysis](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-analysis), and [Part 3](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-3-write-the-surveys-and-the-task-protocol).

## A7-DEC-010: The Test Runs On Staging With `source=usability-test`

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the test runs in person on the university network against the staging deployment, with the seed reset to the appropriate values before each session and no real payment.
  Every test event carries `source=usability-test`; both `smoke` and `usability-test` are test traffic; every metric query excludes both, and the analytics page states why for each metric.
  The test's task funnel is read with the `source=usability-test` filter and shown separately from a metric.
  No new per-participant field is added, because the events' `session_id` is the random analytics session on the booking page and the test uses the source filter and the session's time window.
  Production carries only real traffic.
- **Why:** production is where the release is verified and staging is where a quality requirement is checked, so a test on production would write a participant's booking into the customer's data.
  A participant is not a paying customer, so the test traffic cannot be a metric value; the source filter is the one field the Week 6 event table already carries, and a per-participant link would be a new schema and a new privacy problem.
- **Alternatives considered:** running the test on production with its own source, rejected because it writes the customer's live data; excluding only `smoke`, rejected because the participant's booking then inflates the conversion metric; and a `participant_id` on the events, rejected because the existing `session_id` already groups a booking-page visit and the mapping would be a personal-data join.
- **Where it lands:** [The Events](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md#the-events), [The Dashboard](../../requirements/analytics-requirements.md#the-dashboard), and [Telemetry And Personal Data](../../requirements/visibility-requirements.md#telemetry-and-personal-data).

## A7-DEC-011: The Usability Requirement Is Interaction Capability And Manual

- **Status:** Reversed by [A7-DEC-025](#a7-dec-025-the-response-measure-is-team-chosen-and-real)
- **Decision:** the week adds one `Interaction Capability` quality requirement, with one sub-characteristic the team chooses from `Learnability`, `Self-descriptiveness`, and `Operability`, and one sentence saying why the chosen one is what the test measures; the example uses `Self-descriptiveness`.
  It carries `**Business importance:** High`, so its test is required, and the team's `**Technical risk:**`.
  Its scenario reads a representative participant completing the main usability test task unaided, in the staging deployment with seeded data, and its `Response Measure` is that at least three participants complete the task unaided and the median main-task SEQ is at least five of seven.
  Its `QRT` is `manual`, names `UT-01` in `**Runs at:**`, carries `**Why Manual:**` including that it reads no `SLI`, and needs no deviation.
  Its testing plan row is `Not verified` with `Planned for sprint: SPRINT-08` in Week 7, and `Passes` or `Fails` with `Checked: SPRINT-08` and the results as evidence in Week 8.
  Every other `manual` test keeps the deviation rule.
- **Why:** usability is a quality of the product's interaction, and ISO/IEC 25010:2023 puts it under `Interaction Capability`; the three candidate sub-characteristics are the ones a first-use task can measure, and the team's choice is the one sentence that says which quality the test is about.
  No command can judge whether a person completed a task unaided, so the test is honestly `manual`; making that expected case exempt from the deviation removes a deviation every team would otherwise declare and keeps the deviation for the manual tests that are an escape.
- **Alternatives considered:** `Appropriateness Recognizability` or `User Assistance`, rejected because the test's task is whether a first-time participant can do the main flow, not whether the interface announces itself or helps; a `deployed` test with a script, rejected because the completion is a person's judgement and the script would grade a checklist; and requiring a deviation for the usability test, rejected because it is the one expected manual case and the deviation would stop meaning anything.
- **Where it lands:** [Quality Requirement Tests](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#quality-requirement-tests), [The Quality Requirement](../../requirements/quality-requirements-requirements.md#the-quality-requirement), and [Part 5](../../assignments/assignment-7.md#part-5-write-the-quality-requirement-and-its-manual-test).

## A7-DEC-012: The Findings And The Triage

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** Week 8 records each finding as a `### Finding 1` section with a stable anchor, its severity `Critical`, `Major`, or `Minor`, the task and participant codes it came from, its evidence, its recommendation, and the task issue when one is opened.
  Every finding is triaged; when a significant finding exists, at least one becomes a task issue scheduled in `SPRINT-08`; no finding is forced when the test finds nothing significant.
  A finding that changes product behaviour and a defect are carried by a task issue that links the story and the `AC-nn` and names the `FR-nnn` or `ADR-nnn` in `### Requirements`; a defect never says `None`, and a task that is not product work says `None: repository upkeep`.
  The task issue cites the finding by `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/results.md#finding-1`, and the finding links the issue.
  `REL-08` includes any fix; a fix that cannot finish moves to `SPRINT-09` and the report says so.
- **Why:** a finding with no severity cannot be ordered, and a finding with no task issue is a paragraph the backlog never sees; the story and the requirement links keep the fix in the traceable chain the rest of the course uses, and the finding's anchor is what makes the task issue cite the evidence rather than describe it.
  No finding is forced because a test that finds nothing significant is a result, and a task issue invented to satisfy the rule is work nobody needs.
- **Alternatives considered:** a `FINDING-nn` family, rejected because a finding's anchor is stable and the family would be a second identifier for the same paragraph; forcing one task issue per finding, rejected because a `Minor` finding may be noted and not fixed; and routing the fix as a new functional requirement only, rejected because the requirement does not schedule work and the task issue does.
- **Where it lands:** [The Triage](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-triage), [The Issue Form](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md#the-issue-form), and [The Results And Findings](../../requirements/usability-testing-requirements.md#the-results-and-findings).

## A7-DEC-015: `TOS-03` Stays An Example

- **Status:** Accepted

## A7-DEC-016: The Method Is One New Guide And Three Pointers

## A7-DEC-031: The Analysis Is A Minimum And The QR Reads A Reported Statistic

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision:** the Week 7 analysis plan is a minimum, not a menu: every team reports the per-participant table, the main task's aggregate completion (including the unaided count or proportion), the main task's median time, the median SEQ for each task, the SUS mean and median with `n=<count>`, the thematic analysis, the severity, the telemetry comparison, and the limitations.
  A team may add a measure relevant to its product, provided the plan names the measure, its statistic, and its scope before the first session, and a measure the plan does not name is not added after the data.
  The usability requirement's four instruments are the menu, not the statistic: its `Response Measure` uses the main-task completion or assistance count or proportion, the main-task median time, the main-task median SEQ, or the SUS mean or median, so the verdict reads a number the analysis reports.
  This clarifies [`A7-DEC-029`](#a7-dec-029-the-team-chooses-the-measure) without reversing it: the team still chooses the instrument, and the statistic is the one the analysis reports.
- **Why:** the minimum is the common core a reader can compare and the course can grade, and stating each statistic's scope removes the ambiguity between the main task and all three tasks; the additional-measure clause lets a team ask a product question the course did not anticipate without reopening the measure choice after the data.
  The statistic clause closes the gap where a team could write a mean-SEQ criterion the analysis never reports, which would leave the `QR/QRT` failing on a number no plan committed to.
  The four-way instrument choice stands; only the statistic is fixed, so the sub-characteristic still decides which instrument a team reads.
- **Alternatives considered:** fixing only the SEQ median and leaving the scope and the statistic implicit, rejected because the same ambiguity would remain; letting the team choose the statistic as well as the instrument, rejected because the verdict would leave the analysis core; forbidding additional measures, rejected because a team's product question may need one and the plan is the right place to commit to it; and requiring every measure a team can imagine, rejected because it invents work no team needs.
