## Design Decisions

Week 7 is the design and preparation of the usability test, the full sprint around it, and the handoff to Week 8; the test is not run and its results are not claimed in Week 7.

- **The split.**
  Week 7 writes `UT-01`, its surveys, its consent, its private text repository, its recordings folder, its QR/QRT, and its analysis plan, and it recruits and schedules at least three participants, with five as the target.
  Week 8 runs the sessions, records the raw text in the private repository and the recordings in the folder, writes the sanitized session notes and results, triages the findings, records the QR/QRT result, cuts `REL-08`, and reports the results with Submission 8.
  Week 9 reflects on the results and cites `UT-01`, and prepares the 7-minute deck that Submission 9 carries and the Week 10 class presents; it has no usability results report of its own.
  The split is what makes the test possible in the week it is taught and the results possible by the week they are needed, without a Week 7 report that claims a test it has not run.
- **The test record.**
  `docs/usability-tests/` is a maintained group with an index and one directory per `UT-nn`.
  `docs/usability-tests/README.md` lists each test once with `UT`, `Goal`, `Status`, `Results`, and `Sprints`; it is a finder, not a second record.
  `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/README.md` is the test record: `# UT-01: <title>`, `**Status:**`, `**Design sprint:**`, `**Run sprint:**`, a one-sentence goal, the research questions, the QR it serves, the participant profile, the tasks, the protocol, the measures, the analysis plan, the schedule, the roles, the environment, the consent and data map, the evidence map, and the link to `results.md`.
  The record is public and sanitized; the survey instrument, the consent template, the session template, the sanitized session notes, the results, and the images sit beside it.
  The record is created in Week 7, its status becomes `Completed` in Week 8, and its results are linked rather than copied.
- **The file layout.**
  `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/` carries the record in `README.md`, the instruments in `surveys.md`, the public consent template in `consent.md`, the session template and instructions in `sessions/README.md`, one sanitized note per counted session in `sessions/P<n>.md`, the results in `results.md`, and the sanitized images in `images/`.
  The record owns the goal, the research questions, the participant profile, the tasks, the protocol, the measures, the analysis plan, the schedule, the roles, the environment, and the evidence map.
  There is no separate `tasks.md`, `protocol.md`, or `analysis.md`, because the record is the one place a stranger runs the test from.
- **The participants.**
  At least three completed sessions with people outside the team, and five as the design target: not a teammate, not the customer, not the instructor or the course staff.
  At least three match the participant profile the team records in the test record; the other sessions may be secondary users or people who match the product's buying or usage situation.
  The team records each participant's relation to the target user, familiarity, device, and a local code in the public record; the identity mapping stays in the private repository.
  A pilot or dry run does not count; a session that is withdrawn, not recorded, or not consented to the recording and the sanitized public results does not count; the team recruits a replacement under the next code, and a withdrawn code is retired and never reused.
  The team recruits in Week 7 and schedules the sessions in Week 8; the sessions are booked before the Week 7 deadline.
- **The participant profile.**
  The test record carries a `## Participant Profile` section that states in observable terms who the test needs: the role, that the person books sessions online, their familiarity with similar products, and the device.
  The product vision's `Stakeholders` table is background, not the profile, because it names who the product is for rather than how a reviewer judges that a participant matches.
  A reviewer judges a match from the profile's criteria and the public session note, and the identity mapping stays private.
- **The tasks.**
  One main usability test task and two secondary usability test tasks.
  The main task is the product's main booking flow that the Week 6 event table covers and the QR's subject; it is the same task for every participant, and the materials call it a usability test task.
  The two secondary tasks cover adjacent flows the team wants to learn about, and their order is fixed across participants.
  Every task is a scenario with a goal and a start point: it says what the participant wants to achieve, never which button, label, screen, or step to use, and never how many steps it takes.
  The success criterion is written before the first session: `Unaided` when the participant reaches the defined end state without help, `Assisted` when a hint or help is needed and recorded, and `Failed` when the end state is not reached within the time limit.
  The facilitator reads the scenario verbatim and answers only questions about the scenario, not the interface; the observer records the help, the time, and the friction.
  The dry run with a teammate checks the wording and the time limit, is not evidence, and is not counted.
- **The instruments.**
  Every session records a pre-test background, a think-aloud observation, task completion and assistance, time on task, a SEQ after each task, a SUS after all tasks, a SUS score, and an open-ended post-test.
  The pre-test asks for the participant's role and background, familiarity with similar products, device, and expectations, with no name, email address, phone number, or free-text identification.
  The SEQ is one question after each task on a seven-point scale; the SUS is the standard ten items on the five-point scale, scored `SUS = 2.5 × (20 + sum(odd) - sum(even))`, and the results report its mean and median with the `n=<count>` caveat.
  The QR reads the main task's median SEQ; the results report the per-task SEQ for the main and the two secondary tasks.
  The open-ended post-test asks for the overall impression, what the participant would change, and what the team did not ask.
  The ASQ is optional; the SEQ and the SUS are enough for comparability and the lecture's frameworks.
  The qualitative evidence is the observation notes, the quotes, the friction points, and the think-aloud; the quantitative evidence is the completion, the assistance, the time, the SEQ, the SUS, and the Week 6 analytics events where they cover the task.
  The analysis plan is written before the sessions: a per-participant table, an aggregate completion rate, a median time, a median SEQ, a SUS mean and median, a thematic analysis of the notes, a severity rating, a telemetry comparison, and a limitations section.
- **The consent and the recording.**
  Every counted session has a consent recorded in a durable form before it: a signed form, an email, a Telegram message, or another record the team can show the instructors.
  The consent covers participation, the required recording, the sanitized public results, the anonymized quotes, the private repository, the recordings folder, the instructors' access, the right to withdraw, and the deletion after grading.
  Consent to the recording and to the sanitized public results is mandatory for a session to count; a participant who does not give either cannot take part, and the team recruits a replacement.
  Consent to an anonymized quote is separate and optional; a participant who consents to the recording and the public results but not to a quote still counts, and the team uses the aggregate data and no quote.
  The consent records, the identity mapping, and the raw notes are in the private repository; the recordings are in the cloud folder; the public record holds the template, the sanitized notes, and the sanitized results.
- **The private repository.**
  The team creates one private `<organization>/<repo>-usability-data` repository in Week 7 and adds the team and the instructors as readers.
  It holds text only: `ut-01/consent/P1.md` to `P<n>.md`, `ut-01/participants.csv`, `ut-01/notes/P1.md` to `P<n>.md`, the raw telemetry exports, and the working analysis.
  The recordings live in a team-owned cloud folder, such as the team's drive, shared with the instructors; the private repository carries `ut-01/recordings.md` with the folder link and the access instructions.
  The recordings are never in the product repository and are not in the private repository's history.
  The product repository links the private repository in the test record and the weekly report; the link is excluded from Lychee because it needs authentication, with the reason in `lychee.toml`; the Moodle PDF carries the private repository link and access and the recordings folder link and access.
  The private repository and the recordings stay until the course is graded; after it is graded the team deletes the private repository, its history, and the recordings, and the consent form states this.
  When an instructor cannot access GitHub, the team gives the private text data through a Moodle-only archive and declares the deviation.
- **The evidence map.**
  The public `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/` holds the test record, the surveys, the consent template, the session template, the sanitized session notes, the results, and the sanitized images.
  The private repository holds the text consent records, the identity mapping, the raw notes, the raw telemetry, the recordings index, and the working analysis.
  The cloud folder holds the recordings.
  The Moodle PDF holds the private repository link and access, the recordings folder link and access, the staging address, the staging telemetry credentials, the meeting recording or its reason, the transcript when the customer refused publication, and the privacy line.
  The Week 7 report holds the design summary and its links; the Week 8 report holds the results summary and its links; neither copies the raw data.
- **The quality requirement.**
  The week adds one `Interaction Capability` requirement: a representative participant can complete the main usability test task unaided on first use and finds the task easy.
  The team chooses the sub-characteristic from `Learnability`, `Self-descriptiveness`, and `Operability`, and states in the requirement why the chosen one is what the test measures; the full example uses `Self-descriptiveness`.
  Its scenario source is a representative participant, its stimulus is the main usability test task, its environment is the staging deployment with seeded data, its artifact is the product's interface and the task, its response is the completion and the main task's SEQ, and its response measure is that at least three participants complete the task unaided and the median main-task SEQ is at least five of seven.
  Its `QRT` is `manual`, its `**Runs at:**` names `UT-01` and the test record, and its `**Why Manual:**` says no command can judge whether a representative participant completes the task unaided.
  The `UT-01` record is the inspection procedure; a `manual` usability test naming a `UT-nn` is not a runtime test, reads no `SLI`, and needs no deviation.
  The testing plan row is `Not verified`, `Checked: None`, and `Planned for sprint: SPRINT-08` in Week 7; Week 8 records `Passes` or `Fails`, `Checked: SPRINT-08`, and the `results.md` evidence.
  The QR's `**Business importance:**` is `High` and its `**Technical risk:**` is `Medium`.
  The example's number is the plan's; a team may set a different pre-registered completion count and ease bar, provided the measure is countable, the `QRT` settles it, and the results state it.
- **The analytics.**
  The test runs on the staging deployment with seeded data and no real payment, because production is where the release is verified and staging is where a quality requirement is checked.
  Every test event carries `source=usability-test`; both `smoke` and `usability-test` are test traffic, every metric query excludes both, and the analytics plan states why for each metric.

  OpenPanel is the recommended source for the task funnel; OpenObserve is optional and useful only when the test needs error or latency evidence; neither is required for the pre-test, the SEQ, the SUS, the completion, the time, or the observation.

## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **At least three representative participants cannot be recruited by the Week 7 deadline:** The team recruits replacements, and when three are still not confirmed, the test is moved, the QR/QRT row stays `Not verified` with `Planned for sprint: SPRINT-08` or moves to `SPRINT-09`, the Week 7 report declares the shortfall, and no result is claimed.
- **A participant withdraws or refuses the recording:** The session does not count, the raw text is removed from the private repository and the recording from the folder, the code is retired, and a replacement is recruited under the next code; the Week 8 report says how many counted sessions there were.
- **A participant consents to the session but not to the sanitized public results:** The session is not counted, the team recruits a replacement, and the consent's public-results clause is checked before the session; a participant who consents to the public results but not to an anonymized quote still counts, and the team publishes no quote from that session.
- **A participant is a teammate or the customer:** The session is not counted; the participant is thanked and excluded from the evidence; the participant profile is checked before the session.
- **The private repository or the recordings folder cannot be created or the instructors cannot access it:** The team creates the repository and the folder with the team and the instructors, and when an instructor cannot use GitHub, the team gives the private text data through a Moodle-only archive and declares the deviation; the public sanitized record still stands.
- **The product is broken or unsafe on staging:** The team fixes it or moves the session to the next staging build with a declared deviation; the test plan names the fallback; the test is not presented as a production test.
- **The analytics events do not cover a task:** The team uses the observation, the SEQ, the SUS, the completion, and the time, and says in the results which task the telemetry could not cover; the test does not invent an event or a metric.
- **The `source=usability-test` traffic reaches a metric:** The analytics plan states the exclusion, the query excludes both test sources, and the report does not present the test traffic as a value; when the separation cannot be shown, the report declares it and the metric is not claimed.
- **The SUS or SEQ is incomplete for a session:** The session does not count as a completed session; the missing data is not invented, and a replacement is run.
- **A finding is critical or security-sensitive:** The session stops, the team records the finding, fixes it before the next session when it can, and the results state the stop; the finding is not published with an exploitable detail.
- **A finding is severe and needs a same-week fix:** The team schedules it in `SPRINT-08` and includes the fix in `REL-08`; a fix that cannot finish moves to `SPRINT-09` and the report says so.
- **The test finds nothing significant:** The results say so, the QR/QRT records its verdict, no task issue is forced, and the Week 8 report says the product met the design's bar.
- **The QR/QRT fails:** The testing plan records `Fails` with `Checked: SPRINT-08` and the evidence, the requirement stays active, the findings and the triage carry the fix, and the release claims no quality through it.
- **A session's window is still open at the Week 8 deadline:** The row stays `Not verified` with its `Planned for sprint`, the Week 8 report declares the open window, and the Week 9 reflection records the outcome when it lands.
- **The Week 8 workload is too large:** The Week 8 plan sequences the sessions from 15 to 18 November, the analysis and triage on 19 and 20 November, and the report on 21 November; the test that cannot finish moves the QR/QRT row and the report declares it.
- **A team submits a Week 7 or Week 8 deck PDF or a Week 9 results report anyway:** It is a deviation; the assignment does not require it, the deck is Week 9's and the Week 10 class presents it, and the results report is Submission 8's.
- **A team treats `TOS-03` as required:** The assignment says it is an example; the required check is the QR/QRT; a team without a usability goal is not penalised.
- **A public file would name a participant or a recording:** It does not: the public record uses `P1`, `P2`, and so on, the raw text is in the private repository, the recordings are in the cloud folder, the quote is only with consent, and the screenshot is sanitized.
- **The private repository or the recordings are kept after grading:** The consent form and the plan state the deletion, the team deletes the repository, its history, and the recordings after the course is graded, and the sanitized public record stays.
- **A coding agent is given the raw private data:** The public record is written from the sanitized session note; the agent is given the sanitized note, not the raw recording or the private notes, and the team reviews what it writes before committing.
- **The test design is mistaken for a UAT:** The assignment and the guide call it a usability test; no `**Checked UATs:**` field is added; the UAT item stays in `TODO.md`.
