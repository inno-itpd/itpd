## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **The Sprint 9 review slips past the deadline.**
  The review is moved into the week before the deadline; the Week 9 report declares the slip and still submits by `2026-11-28` 23:59; a review after the submission is a missing deliverable for Submission 9 and a deviation for Submission 10.
- **The Sprint 10 handoff slips past the deadline.**
  The handoff is moved earlier; a handoff after `2026-12-03` 23:59 is a missing deliverable; the Week 10 report declares the slip, and the submission still goes in.
- **The handoff finds something that needs product code.**
  The team agrees the correction at the handoff, applies it before `REL-10`, and cuts `REL-10` on the corrected state before `2026-12-03` 23:59; if it cannot land, it becomes an open item in the Week 10 report's `## Handoff`, a risk, and a task for the next project, and the final report says so; the customer's acceptance may be conditional, and the `DEC-nnn` records the condition.
- **The customer rejects the handoff.**
  The Sprint 10 meeting report records the rejection and the open items, the customer's decision is a `DEC-nnn`, and the Week 10 report's `## Handoff` says what was not accepted; no product code changes after `REL-10`, and the submission still goes in.
- **`REL-10` is not ready before the handoff.**
  The handoff presents the final candidate and the customer agrees the release is cut after the meeting; the meeting report and the Week 10 report record the tag and any delta; `REL-10` is still cut before `2026-12-03` 23:59.
- **A correction lands after `REL-10`.**
  It does not: product code is frozen at `REL-10`; a correction that cannot land is an open item, not a second final release; a hotfix after `REL-10` is not made, and if one is made it is a declared deviation and the final state is not the handoff version.
- **The final report or the reflection is not ready for Submission 10.**
  The Week 10 report and the reflection are required deliverables; a missing one is a missing deliverable, not a deviation; the team writes the Week 10 report from the evidence and the reflection from the retrospectives, and the deadline is not moved.
- **The deck is not ready for Submission 10.**
  The final deck is a required deliverable; a missing final deck is a missing deliverable, not a deviation; the draft from Submission 9 is the basis, and the team finalizes it before the submission.
- **The final exam uses a newer deck than the submitted one.**
  The submitted final deck is the basis; the final exam may use the refined one; the code freeze keeps the demonstrated product stable; the Week 10 report does not link the deck, and the Moodle submission carries the submitted PDF.
- **An issue cannot close before the final state.**
  It is closed as not planned with a reason and recorded as an open item in the Week 10 report's `## Handoff`, or it is recorded as an open item without a false close; the `SPRINT-10` milestone closes after it, and there is no later sprint.
- **The final retrospective has no next retrospective.**
  It says there is no later sprint; its new actions are `Carried: No`; its `## Carried Actions` reports the Sprint 9 actions; its lessons go to `reflection.md`.
- **The private data is deleted before grading.**
  It is not: the private repository and the recordings folder stay accessible to the team and the instructors until the course is graded, and the handoff records the deletion date; the Week 10 report and the reflection record the retention.
- **The final report names an address, a credential, or a person.**
  The public report is sanitized: the address and the credential stay in the wrapper, the person is named by GitHub username, and the Week 10 report, the reflection, and the handoff report remove the value; a leaked credential follows the incident response.
- **The Week 9 report is rewritten after Submission 9.**
  It is not: it is the record of Sprint 9 and Submission 9; the Week 10 report and the reflection carry the later state and cite it.
- **The 107% total is read as a 100% scale.**
  The syllabus states the possible total and the pass threshold: ten group submissions at 7% (70%), 10% attendance, 7% individual reflection, and 20% final exam, for a possible 107%, with the pass threshold at 75%.
