# Plan: Assignments 9 And 10 — The Final Two Sprints, The Two Submissions, The UATs, The Handoff, And The Reflection

This plan designs Assignment 9 and Assignment 10 as [`a8-plan.md`](a8-plan.md) designs Assignment 8: two sprints, `SPRINT-09` and `SPRINT-10`, two graded group submissions, two releases `REL-09` and `REL-10`, the three user acceptance tests, the handoff, the final report, the project reflection, the individual reflection and peer evaluation, the final repository state, the two weekly reports, the two retrospectives, the two AI usage reports, and the two Moodle wrappers.
It states the scope and the contradictions the wider materials expose, with the resolution each takes, in [Scope And Contradictions](#scope-and-contradictions).
The final exam is a separate course event in the syllabus's Week 11 section; this plan does not design it, and no assignment part depends on it.

The Week 9 class dates stay as they are: 20–26 November.
`SPRINT-09` and Submission 9 are due **Sunday 29 November 2026 at 23:59**, and the Sprint 9 review may be held by that due date, including the days after the class week.
The Week 10 class dates stay as they are: 27 November–3 December.
`SPRINT-10` starts on **Monday 30 November** and is due **Thursday 3 December 2026 at 23:59**; Submission 10 and the Sprint 10 review and handoff are due by that date.
A sprint closes after its review and its release, at the latest on its due date.

`REL-09` is cut at the Sprint 9 close.
`REL-10` is a usual product release, cut after the Sprint 10 handoff and any corrections the customer agrees, and before `2026-12-03` 23:59.
Its tag is on the final product commit.
Product code freezes at `REL-10`, and the public repository and the product take no change of any kind after `2026-12-03` 23:59; the post-grading private-data deletion and the approved privatisation are the only later changes.
The main repository is public during the course; after the course has been fully graded, the team may make it private only with the instructors' approval.
The private usability-data repository and the recordings folder stay private until the course has been fully graded, and the team deletes them after grading.

The course has ten graded group submissions: Submission 1 to Submission 10, each worth 7 points, so 70 of the 107 points.
The other components are 10 points of class attendance, 7 points of individual reflection and peer evaluation, and 20 points of final exam, for 107 points in total.
The pass threshold is **75 points of the 107**.
The late submission policy is charged in points: 10% of the submission's points per calendar day after the hard deadline, so a 7-point submission has a maximum of 6.3 points one day late, and a submission more than 7 days late receives zero.
The individual reflection and peer evaluation is Submission 11, due `2026-12-05` 23:59, available only to a member of a group that presented at the final exam, private and Moodle-only, in the syllabus's Week 11 section.
Repository upkeep is assessed inside the ten submissions; it is not a separate component.
Submission 9 and Submission 10 are separate.
The assignment catalog separates the ten group submissions from the final exam, Submission 11, and attendance; the final exam and attendance are not submissions, and Submission 11 is the individual reflection and peer evaluation.

The three user acceptance tests are `UAT-01` to `UAT-03`, maintained at `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md` with the `UAT-nn` identifier family, per the new `requirements/user-acceptance-tests-requirements.md`.
Each test covers one end-to-end user journey, and its section states the journey on the first line, then `**Status:**`, then the start state, the steps, and the expected result, then a runs table.
The runs table has one row per run: the sprint, the date, `Customer` as the runner, the build, the result, the evidence, and the `DEC-nnn`.
The customer runs the three tests on the product deployed to the university VM at the Sprint 9 review, and re-runs them on the release candidate at the Sprint 10 handoff.
There is no third run and no post-handoff run.
The team records what passed and what did not, and it declares any difference between the state the customer ran and the released state.
The three `UAT-nn` replace the core-task verification on the `REL-09` and `REL-10` production deploys; the Weeks 5–8 production deploys keep the core-task rule.
The assignments for Weeks 9 and 10 do not mention the core task.
The `UT-01` results are not re-presented in the final two weeks; the reports link the Week 8 report only where the evidence needs it.

Submission 9 carries the Week 9 report, the Sprint 9 review, the UAT run and its results, the handoff preparation, `REL-09`, the Sprint 9 retrospective, and the AI usage report.
Submission 10 carries the Week 10 report and final report, the Sprint 10 review and handoff, the UAT re-run and its results, `REL-10`, the project reflection, the final repository state, the Sprint 10 retrospective, and the AI usage report.
The pitch deck is a separate Moodle submission, due `2026-12-03` 23:59, described by [`exam/README.md`](../../exam/README.md).
That page is added blank by this plan and is designed later; this plan does not design the deck content or the peer review rules.
The deck is not shown at the Sprint 10 review, and neither Assignment 9 nor Assignment 10 mentions the deck or the peer review.
The deck is never committed to the repository.

The final report is `reports/week-10/README.md`: the Week 10 weekly report and the final project report.
The project reflection is `reports/week-10/reflection.md`: the lessons learned, the problems that taught them, what the team would do differently if it started the project anew, and no team dynamics section.
The individual reflection and peer evaluation is Submission 11, private and Moodle-only, submitted after the final exam; the syllabus's Week 11 section carries it.
There is no `reports/week-10/final-report.md`.
The handoff is `reports/week-10/handoff.md`, the handoff report; the Week 10 report's `## Handoff results` section links it.
The Week 9 report carries `## Handoff preparation`, written after the Sprint 9 review from the customer's answer to what they expect to receive as the handoff; the Sprint 9 meeting script asks that question.

It carries no rule 17 table, no proposal column, no focus metric, and no link to `docs/analytics.md` or `docs/observability.md`.
It requires no screenshots.

## Design Decisions

Week 9 and Week 10 are two sprints, two submissions, two reviews, two releases, one final handoff, one final report, one project reflection, one individual reflection, one final state, one `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md`, and three customer-run `UAT-nn`.

- **The two sprints and the two submissions.**
  Week 9's class dates stay 20–26 November; `SPRINT-09` and Submission 9 are due `2026-11-29`, and the Sprint 9 review may be held by that due date.
  Week 10's class dates stay 27 November–3 December; `SPRINT-10` starts `2026-11-30` and is due `2026-12-03`; Submission 10 is due `2026-12-03`; each submission has its own Moodle wrapper and assignment page.
  The Sprint 10 review and handoff are held by `2026-12-03`.
  `SPRINT-10`'s milestone and its `### SPRINT-10` section with a provisional goal are created in Week 9; it is planned in Week 10.
  The individual reflection and peer evaluation is Submission 11, due `2026-12-05` 23:59, available only to a member of a group that presented at the final exam; it stays individual, private, and Moodle-only, and the syllabus's Week 11 section carries it.
  The course has ten group submissions at 7 points (70), 10 points attendance, 7 points individual reflection and peer evaluation, and 20 points final exam, for 107 points; the pass threshold is 75 points of the 107; the late penalty is 10% of the submission's points per calendar day.
  The assignment catalog separates the ten group submissions from the final exam, Submission 11, and attendance, so no item number competes with a submission number.
  The final exam is a separate event in the syllabus's Week 11 section.
- **Sprint 9.**
  `SPRINT-09` is planned like every other sprint: its provisional goal is replaced by a planned goal, its `**Sprint Backlog:**` links its GitHub milestone, its `**Planned Velocity:**` counts the user stories it schedules, and its `**Actual Velocity:**` is set at the close.
  The UAT tasks, the handoff preparation, the Week 9 report, the threshold review, the retrospective, and the AI usage report are tasks with no story points; no deck task is scheduled; a product story carried from `SPRINT-08` or added in Week 9 counts.
  Week 9's planning refines the `SPRINT-10` backlog only, because there is no later sprint after it.
  The sprint's review is the Week 9 weekly customer meeting, held in person by Sunday 29 November; it shows the Sprint 9 increment, the customer runs the three `UAT-nn` on the product deployed to the university VM, and its script asks what the customer expects to receive as the handoff.
  `REL-09` is cut at the close, after the review and any same-week rework; the Week 9 report, the handoff preparation, the UAT results, the threshold review, and the retrospective are submitted by `2026-11-29` 23:59.
  Every open issue at the close moves to `SPRINT-10`, because the later sprint exists.
- **Sprint 10.**
  `SPRINT-10` is the final sprint; it is planned in Week 10 like every other sprint, and it may carry user stories or only tasks.
  Its goal is a result, not a list of artifacts; its `**Sprint Backlog:**` links its GitHub milestone; its `**Planned Velocity:**` counts the user stories it schedules, and a task-only sprint has `0` story points and says so.
  The final sprint refines only its own backlog, because there are no next two sprints to refine for.
  It sits outside every `MLS-nn` run, which the planning rule allows.
  Its review is the Week 10 weekly customer meeting and the handoff, held by `2026-12-03`; it shows the final candidate and the handoff package, and the customer re-runs the three `UAT-nn` on the release candidate.
  The sprint closes after the review, the customer's corrections, and `REL-10`; it does not close at the handoff.
  At the close, the issues completed in the sprint stay in the closed `SPRINT-10` milestone; every open issue, including the ones scheduled in the sprint and not completed, stays open with no milestone, clears its `Responsible`, `Accountable`, and assignees, and is listed with its link and why it is open in `reports/week-10/handoff.md`; there is no later sprint.
- **The reviews and the handoff.**
  There are two Sprint Reviews, one per sprint: the Week 9 customer meeting reviews Sprint 9, and the Week 10 customer meeting reviews Sprint 10 and hands the product over.
  Both reviews are in person.
  The Week 9 review's script is written before the meeting and asks what the customer expects to receive as the handoff, alongside the questions the Sprint 9 increment needs; the review shows the increment, the customer runs the three `UAT-nn` on the VM deployment, and the review records the customer's verdict on the Sprint 9 stories as `DEC-nnn` entries and the UAT set as one `DEC-nnn`.
  The Week 9 report's `## Handoff preparation` is written after the review: it records the customer's expectations, what is ready, and what is missing, and it reports the UAT results.
  The Week 10 review shows the final candidate and the handoff package, the customer re-runs the three `UAT-nn` on the release candidate, and the review records the final story verdicts, the UAT re-run as a separate `DEC-nnn`, and the handoff acceptance as a `DEC-nnn`.
  The Week 10 review and the handoff are one meeting with one script written before it and one meeting report written after it, `reports/week-10/meeting-report.md`; there is no second meeting and no `meeting-report-2.md`.
  `reports/week-10/handoff.md` is the handoff report: one row per item, its status, and where it lives, with public rows linking public evidence and private rows saying the value is in the Moodle wrapper's `## For customer`, plus the link to the Week 9 preparation and the citation of the meeting report for the customer's verdict (acceptance, conditional acceptance, or refusal) and the UAT re-run.
  The Week 10 report's `## Handoff results` links the handoff file and states the customer's verdict.
  The handoff's rows are the MVP and `REL-10`, the repository, the deployed MVP, the UAT re-run, the documentation, the dashboards and access, the credentials, the known issues, the open issues, the private-data retention and deletion, and the instructors' access to the private usability data.
  The handoff is live; the asynchronous rule is a last resort with the written exchange as the transcript and a declared deviation.
- **The user acceptance tests.**
  The team defines `UAT-01` to `UAT-03` in `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md` before the Sprint 9 review; each covers one end-to-end user journey, and its section states the journey on the first line, then `**Status:**`, then the start state, the steps, the expected result, and a runs table.
  The runs table has one row per run: the sprint, the date, `Customer` as the runner, the build, the result, the evidence, and the `DEC-nnn`.
  The evidence is the sanitized meeting report's anchor, and the `DEC-nnn` is the run's decision; a run is cited as the test's section plus the sprint, such as `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md#uat-01` and `SPRINT-09`.
  The team defines exactly three tests, one per end-to-end journey it chooses.
  The customer runs the three tests on the product deployed to the university VM at the Sprint 9 review; the team records each pass or fail; the customer's verdict on the set is one `DEC-nnn`.
  The customer re-runs the three tests on the release candidate at the Sprint 10 handoff; the re-run is a separate `DEC-nnn` on top of the handoff acceptance; there is no third run and no post-handoff run.
  A product correction the handoff agrees may land before `REL-10`; if it changes a journey, the Week 10 report declares the difference between the state the customer ran and the released state.
  Both runs are recorded in `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md`; the Week 9 report links its run, the Week 10 report links its re-run, and the final report cites the results.
  The three `UAT-nn` replace the core-task verification on both the `REL-09` and `REL-10` production deploys; the Weeks 5–8 deploys keep the core-task rule; the assignments do not mention the core task.
  The `UT-01` results are not re-presented in the final two weeks; the reports link the Week 8 report only where the evidence needs it.
  The UATs are a new requirements file and a new identifier family; they are not a `QRT-nn`, and the UAT record is `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md`.
- **The releases, the MVP, and the code freeze.**
  `REL-09` is the Week 9 release, cut at the Sprint 9 close on the final Sprint 9 product commit, after the UAT run and the review's rework.
  `REL-10` is a usual product release and the MVP, cut after the handoff and any corrections the customer agrees, on the final product commit and before `2026-12-03` 23:59.
  The handoff presents the final candidate; the team applies the corrections the customer agrees; `REL-10` is cut on the corrected state; the Week 10 report records the final tag and commit.
  The release bodies are the usual upgrade notes; the `REL-10` body names the release and says whether the upgrade needs anything beyond deploying.
  The plan's `#### REL-09` and `#### REL-10` blocks carry `**Released:**`, `**Checked QRs:**`, and `**Checked UATs:**`; those two blocks are the only ones that carry the UAT field, and a sprint with no checked requirement says `None`.
  The `**Checked UATs:**` line links the test's section and the sprint's run, and the field's scope is the `REL-09` and `REL-10` blocks only.
  Product code freezes at `REL-10`; docs, the reports, the reflection, the changelog, and the release blocks may merge between `REL-10` and `2026-12-03` 23:59; no public change of any kind is made after `2026-12-03` 23:59.
  The main repository is public during the course; after the course is fully graded, the team may make it private only with the instructors' approval; the private usability-data repository and the recordings are private, and the team deletes them after the course is fully graded; that mandatory deletion and the approved privatisation are the only later changes.
  The changelog's released sections, the `REL-nn` blocks, and the reports are merged after their tags, as the release rule already orders them.
- **The verification.**
  The `REL-09` and `REL-10` production deploys are verified by the customer-run three `UAT-nn` on the deployed candidate: the Sprint 9 run for `REL-09` and the Sprint 10 re-run on the release candidate for `REL-10`.
  The team deploys the current candidate to the production VM before each review and shows it live.
  The team records what passed and what did not.
  A product correction may land before `REL-10`; if it changes a journey, the Week 10 report declares the difference between the state the customer ran and the released state, and the final report does not claim the released state was customer-tested.
  The Week 9 report links its run; the Week 10 report links its re-run; `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md` is the record; the handoff's deployment row points at the re-run.
  The Weeks 5–8 production deploys keep the core-task verification, per [Environments And Deployment](../../requirements/repository-requirements.md#environments-and-deployment).
  A failed `UAT-nn` at the Sprint 9 review is fixed before `REL-09` or becomes a known issue or an open item; a failed `UAT-nn` at the Sprint 10 re-run is recorded as `Fail`; there is no post-handoff run, and the handoff acceptance is conditional or refused.

## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **The Sprint 9 review is held on Sunday 29 November.**
  The review is in person, so it is a daytime meeting; the rework, `REL-09`, the Week 9 report, and the wrapper then have to finish before 23:59.
  A submission that misses the deadline is late under the syllabus's late policy, and the Week 9 report declares the compressed schedule.
  A review after the deadline is a missing deliverable for Submission 9 and a deviation for Submission 10, and the Week 10 report declares the slip; the Week 9 report is not rewritten.
- **The Sprint 10 review is held on Thursday 3 December.**
  The review is in person, so it is a daytime meeting; the corrections, `REL-10`, the changelog and `REL-10` block, the Week 10 report, the final `main` commit, and the wrapper then have to finish before 23:59.
  The Week 10 report cannot be finalized before the meeting, so a late meeting makes Submission 10 late; the wrapper and the final report say so, and the late policy applies.
  A handoff after the deadline is a missing deliverable, and the submission still goes in late.
- **The handoff finds something that needs product code.**
  The team agrees the correction at the handoff, applies it before `REL-10`, and cuts `REL-10` on the corrected state before `2026-12-03` 23:59; the Week 10 report declares the difference between the UAT run and the release; if the correction cannot land, it becomes an open item in `reports/week-10/handoff.md`, a risk, and a task for the next project, and the final report says so; the customer's verdict may be conditional, and the `DEC-nnn` records the condition.
- **The customer rejects the handoff.**
  The Sprint 10 meeting report records the rejection and the open items, the customer's decision is a `DEC-nnn`, and `reports/week-10/handoff.md` says what was not accepted; no product code changes after `REL-10`, and the submission still goes in.
- **`REL-10` is not ready before the handoff.**
  The handoff presents the final candidate and the customer agrees the release is cut after the meeting; the Week 10 report records the tag and any delta; `REL-10` is still cut after the handoff and before `2026-12-03` 23:59.
- **A correction lands after `REL-10`.**
  It does not: product code is frozen at `REL-10`; a correction that cannot land is an open item, not a second final release; a hotfix after `REL-10` is not made, and if one is made it is a declared deviation and the final state is not the handoff version.
- **A UAT fails at the Sprint 9 review.**
  The team fixes it before `REL-09` if the fix fits the same week, or it becomes a known issue or an open item; the customer's `DEC-nnn` for the set records the failure, the Week 9 report reports it, and the final report does not claim that journey passes.
- **A UAT fails at the Sprint 10 re-run.**
  The Sprint 10 meeting report records the failure, the separate re-run `DEC-nnn` records it, the handoff acceptance is conditional or refused, `reports/week-10/handoff.md` says so, and the final report says the MVP claim is narrower; there is no post-handoff re-run.
- **The Sprint 9 review's rework changes a UAT journey.**
  The Week 9 report declares the delta; there is no third run, and the Sprint 10 re-run covers the final state.
- **The customer cannot run a UAT.**
  The tests are customer-run, so the team does not substitute for the customer; the run is rescheduled inside the review window, or the missing run is a missing deliverable and a declared deviation, and the final report claims no pass for that journey.
- **A UAT journey changes between the two runs.**
  The updated test is recorded in `docs/user-acceptance-tests.md`, the two runs are kept with their dates and builds, and the Week 10 report declares the difference.
- **The `REL-09` or `REL-10` deploy is verified by the core task.**
  It is not: the three `UAT-nn` replace the core-task verification on those two deploys, and the Weeks 5–8 deploys keep the core-task rule; a core-task check on `REL-09` or `REL-10` is a declared deviation.
- **The final report or the project reflection is not ready for Submission 10.**
  The Week 10 report and the project reflection are required deliverables; a missing one is a missing deliverable, not a deviation; the team writes the Week 10 report from the evidence and the reflection from the project, and the deadline is not moved.
- **The deck is not ready for its separate submission.**
  The deck is a separate Moodle submission due `2026-12-03` 23:59 and is not in Submission 10; a missing deck is a missing deliverable for that submission, not for Submission 10; `exam/README.md` is blank and designed later.
- **The final exam uses a newer deck than the submitted one.**
  The final exam is a separate event in the syllabus's Week 11 section; this plan submits the deck by `2026-12-03` 23:59 and does not design it; a change to the deck for the exam is outside this plan.
- **An issue cannot close before the final state.**
  The issues completed in `SPRINT-10` stay in its closed milestone; an issue scheduled in `SPRINT-10` and not completed leaves the milestone, stays open with no milestone, clears its `Responsible`, `Accountable`, and assignees, and is listed with its link and why it is open in `reports/week-10/handoff.md`; there is no later sprint.
- **The final retrospective has no next retrospective.**
  It says there is no later sprint; a `Not done` action is `Outcome: Not done` in `## Carried Actions`, and the Sprint 10 retrospective's `## Actions` carry `Carried: No`, and nothing is carried into the project reflection; the reflection is the project's lessons, not the retrospective's next home.
- **The private data is deleted before grading.**
  It is not: the private repository and the recordings folder stay private and accessible to the team and the instructors until the course is fully graded; the team deletes them after grading, and the handoff and the wrappers' `## For customer` record the retention and the mandatory deletion; the Week 10 report and the reflection record the retention.
- **The final report names an address, a credential, or a person.**
  The public report is sanitized: the address and the credential stay in the wrappers' `## For customer`, the person is named by GitHub username, and the Week 10 report, the reflection, and the handoff report remove the value; a leaked credential follows the incident response.
- **The Week 9 report is rewritten after Submission 9.**
  It is not: it is the record of Sprint 9 and Submission 9; the Week 10 report and the reflection carry the later state and cite it.
- **The 107 points are read as a 100-point scale.**

- **The MVP claim overstates what was verified.**
  The final report cites the three `UAT-nn` results and the customer's verdict (acceptance, conditional acceptance, or refusal); it does not cite the focus metric; a failed or missed journey is a known issue or an open item, and the report does not claim a journey it did not check.
