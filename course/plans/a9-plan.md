## Goal And Success Criteria

## Design Decisions

Week 9 and Week 10 are two sprints, two submissions, two reviews, two releases, one final handoff, one final report, one project reflection, one individual reflection, and one final state.

- **The two sprints and the two submissions.**
  `SPRINT-09` runs in Week 9 and is due `2026-11-28`; `SPRINT-10` runs in Week 10 and is due `2026-12-03`.
  Submission 9 is due `2026-11-28`; Submission 10 is due `2026-12-03`; each is a Moodle submission and each has its own assignment page.
  The individual reflection and peer evaluation is Submission 11, due `2026-12-05` 23:59, available only to a member of a group that presented at the final exam; it stays individual, private, and Moodle-only.
  The course has ten group submissions at 7 points (70), 10 points attendance, 7 points individual reflection and peer evaluation, and 20 points final exam, for 107 points; the pass threshold is 75 points.
- **Sprint 9.**
  `SPRINT-09` is planned like every other sprint: its provisional goal is replaced by a planned goal, its `**Sprint Backlog:**` links its GitHub milestone, its `**Planned Velocity:**` counts the user stories it schedules, and its `**Actual Velocity:**` is set at the close.
  The handoff preparation, the final report draft, the Week 9 report, the retrospective, and the AI usage report are tasks with no story points; no deck task is scheduled; a product story carried from `SPRINT-08` or added in Week 9 counts.
  `SPRINT-10` is created in Week 9, when Sprint 10 is planned, so the seven milestones created in Week 3 grow by one in the week that needs it.
  The sprint's review is the Week 9 weekly customer meeting, held by Saturday 28 November, with a same-day review allowed; it shows the Sprint 9 increment and the final report draft, and its script asks what the customer expects to receive as the handoff.
  `REL-09` is cut at the close, after the review and any same-week rework; the Week 9 report, the handoff preparation, and the retrospective are submitted by `2026-11-28` 23:59.
  Every open issue at the close moves to `SPRINT-10`, because the later sprint exists.
- **Sprint 10.**
  `SPRINT-10` is the final sprint; it is planned like every other sprint, and it may carry user stories or only tasks.
  Its goal is a result, not a list of artifacts; its `**Sprint Backlog:**` links its GitHub milestone; its `**Planned Velocity:**` counts the user stories it schedules, and a task-only sprint has `0` story points and says so.
  The final sprint refines only its own backlog, because there are no next two sprints to refine for.
  Its review is the Week 10 weekly customer meeting and the handoff; the review and the handoff are one meeting, held by Thursday 3 December, with a same-day review allowed; the review shows the final candidate, the final deck, the Week 10 report draft, and the handoff package.
  The sprint closes after the review, the customer's corrections, and `REL-10`; it does not close at the handoff.
  At the close, the issues completed in the sprint stay in the closed `SPRINT-10` milestone; every open issue, including the ones scheduled in the sprint and not completed, stays open with no milestone and is listed with its link and why it is open in the Week 10 report's `## Handoff`; there is no later sprint.
- **The reviews and the handoff.**
  There are two Sprint Reviews, one per sprint: the Week 9 customer meeting reviews Sprint 9, and the Week 10 customer meeting reviews Sprint 10 and hands the product over.
  The Week 9 review's script is written before the meeting and asks what the customer expects to receive as the handoff, alongside the questions the Sprint 9 increment needs; the review shows the increment and the final report draft and records the customer's verdict on the Sprint 9 stories as `DEC-nnn` entries.
  The Week 9 report's `## Handoff preparation` is written after the review: it records the customer's expectations, what is ready, and what is missing.
  The Week 10 review shows the final candidate, the final deck, the Week 10 report draft, and the handoff package, and records the final story verdicts and the handoff acceptance as `DEC-nnn` entries.
  The Week 10 review and the handoff are one meeting with one script written before it and one meeting report written after it, `reports/week-10/meeting-report.md`; there is no second meeting and no `meeting-report-2.md`.
  The Week 10 report's `## Handoff` is the handoff report: one row per item, its status, and where it lives, with public rows linking public evidence and private rows pointing at the Moodle wrapper's `## For customer`, plus the link to the Week 9 preparation and the citation of the meeting report for the acceptance.
  The handoff is live; the asynchronous rule is a last resort with the written exchange as the transcript and a declared deviation.
  The handoff records the `REL-10` release or the final candidate, the repository, the deployed MVP, the documentation, the dashboards and access, the credentials, the known issues, the open items, the private-data retention and deletion date, and the customer's access; the private values are in the wrapper.
- **The releases, the MVP, and the code freeze.**
  `REL-09` is the Week 9 release, cut at the Sprint 9 close on the final Sprint 9 product commit.
  `REL-10` is the final release and the MVP, cut before `2026-12-03` 23:59 on the corrected final state, before or after the handoff.
  If `REL-10` is ready before the handoff, the handoff cites it; if it is not, the handoff report records that the release is to be cut after the meeting and the Week 10 report records the final tag and commit.
  The release bodies are the handoff and upgrade notes: what the release is, how to run it, what is known, whether the upgrade requires anything beyond deploying, and the `[Full changelog]` line that closes it; the `REL-10` body names the release as the MVP.
  The plan's `#### REL-09` and `#### REL-10` blocks carry `**Released:**` and `**Checked QRs:**`; a sprint with no checked requirement says `None` and keeps the testing-plan sentence; a final release with no product change says so and requires no upgrade.
  Product code freezes at `REL-10`; docs, the reports, the reflection, the changelog, and the release blocks may merge between `REL-10` and `2026-12-03` 23:59; no public change of any kind is made after `2026-12-03` 23:59.
  The private usability-data repository and the recordings folder stay with the team and the instructors until the course is graded and are deleted after it; that deletion is the only post-freeze change.
  The changelog's released sections, the `REL-nn` blocks, and the reports are merged after their tags, as the release rule already orders them.
- **The final production verification.**
  The final production deployment is the `REL-10` commit, deployed from staging from a machine on the university network.
  The verification checks the MVP's main user journeys on the deployed product, not the Week 3 MUP's core task, and a journey that would block use is checked end to end.
  The Week 10 report records the checklist and the result; the `## Handoff` deployment row points at it; the release body stays the upgrade note and does not carry the check.
  A failed journey is fixed before `REL-10`, or it becomes a known issue or an open item and the final report says the MVP claim is narrower.
- **The reports and the final report.**
  `reports/week-09/README.md` is the Week 9 report and the index of Submission 9; it carries the summary, `## Handoff preparation`, the coverage table, the contribution table, the repository evidence, the deviations, and the privacy line; it links the Sprint 9 review, `REL-09`, the retrospective, and the AI usage report; it names no deck.
  `reports/week-10/README.md` is the Week 10 report, the final project report, and the index of Submission 10; it carries the summary, the final state, `## Handoff`, `## Threshold review`, the engineering journey, the technical choices, the outcomes, the MVP, the quality goals achieved and missed, the coverage table, the contribution table, the repository evidence, the deviations, and the privacy line; it links the Sprint 10 review and handoff, `REL-10`, the retrospective, the AI usage report, and the reflection; it names the final deck as submitted on Moodle, and it records the `REL-10` tag and its commit.
  The Week 10 report is an exception to the weekly report's index role: it is the final report and the index, and it does not repeat the evidence it links.
  `## Threshold review` names each `TOS-nn` that is `Missed` or `Open` and each `QRT-nn` row that is `Fails`, `Not verified`, or `Dropped`, with a one-line reason, and links the threshold and the testing plan; it copies no row and carries no full status table.
  The Week 9 report and the Week 10 report are separate records; no file in `reports/week-09/` is rewritten after Submission 9.
  There is no `reports/week-10/final-report.md`.
- **The project reflection and the individual reflection.**
  `reports/week-10/reflection.md` is the project reflection: the lessons learned and the problems that taught them, what the team would do differently in the next project, and the team dynamics.
  It is public and sanitized, names no private address and no credential, and rates no individual; the weekly reports' contribution tables are the evidence of who did what.
  It draws on the `SPRINT-04` to `SPRINT-10` retrospectives, the risk register, the `UT-01` results, and the configuration plan; it is not the final retrospective's carry-forward, and it does not re-list the retrospective's actions.
  Submission 11 is the individual reflection and peer evaluation, private and Moodle-only, due `2026-12-05` 23:59, and available only to a member of a group that presented at the final exam; the Week 10 report links the project reflection and does not link Submission 11.
- **The deck.**
  The pitch deck is a 7-minute product pitch.

## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **The Sprint 9 review slips past the deadline.**
  The review is held by `2026-11-28` 23:59, with a same-day review allowed; a review after the submission is a missing deliverable for Submission 9 and a deviation for Submission 10, and the Week 9 report declares the slip.
- **The Sprint 10 handoff slips past the deadline.**
  The handoff is held by `2026-12-03` 23:59, with a same-day review allowed; a handoff after the deadline is a missing deliverable; the Week 10 report declares the slip, and the submission still goes in.
- **The handoff finds something that needs product code.**
  The team agrees the correction at the handoff, applies it before `REL-10`, and cuts `REL-10` on the corrected state before `2026-12-03` 23:59; if it cannot land, it becomes an open item in the Week 10 report's `## Handoff`, a risk, and a task for the next project, and the final report says so; the customer's acceptance may be conditional, and the `DEC-nnn` records the condition.
- **The customer rejects the handoff.**
  The Sprint 10 meeting report records the rejection and the open items, the customer's decision is a `DEC-nnn`, and the Week 10 report's `## Handoff` says what was not accepted; no product code changes after `REL-10`, and the submission still goes in.
- **`REL-10` is not ready before the handoff.**
  The handoff presents the final candidate and the customer agrees the release is cut after the meeting; the meeting report and the Week 10 report record the tag and any delta; `REL-10` is still cut before `2026-12-03` 23:59.
- **A correction lands after `REL-10`.**
  It does not: product code is frozen at `REL-10`; a correction that cannot land is an open item, not a second final release; a hotfix after `REL-10` is not made, and if one is made it is a declared deviation and the final state is not the handoff version.
- **The final report or the project reflection is not ready for Submission 10.**
  The Week 10 report and the project reflection are required deliverables; a missing one is a missing deliverable, not a deviation; the team writes the Week 10 report from the evidence and the reflection from the project, and the deadline is not moved.
- **The pitch deck is not ready for Submission 10.**
  The one deck is a required deliverable; a missing deck is a missing deliverable, not a deviation, and there is no draft from Submission 9 to fall back on; the team finalizes it before the submission.
- **The final exam uses a newer deck than the submitted one.**
  The submitted final deck is the basis; the final exam may use the refined one; the code freeze keeps the demonstrated product stable; the Week 10 report does not link the deck, and the Moodle submission carries the submitted PDF.
- **An issue cannot close before the final state.**
  The issues completed in `SPRINT-10` stay in its closed milestone; an issue scheduled in `SPRINT-10` and not completed leaves the milestone, stays open with no milestone, and is listed with its link and why it is open in the Week 10 report's `## Handoff`; there is no later sprint.
- **The final retrospective has no next retrospective.**
  It says there is no later sprint; a `Not done` action is `Carried: No` with its reason, and nothing is carried into the project reflection; the reflection is the project's lessons, not the retrospective's next home.
- **The private data is deleted before grading.**
  It is not: the private repository and the recordings folder stay accessible to the team and the instructors until the course is graded, and the handoff and the wrapper's `## For customer` record the deletion date; the Week 10 report and the reflection record the retention.
- **The final report names an address, a credential, or a person.**
  The public report is sanitized: the address and the credential stay in the wrapper's `## For customer`, the person is named by GitHub username, and the Week 10 report, the reflection, and the handoff report remove the value; a leaked credential follows the incident response.
- **The Week 9 report is rewritten after Submission 9.**
  It is not: it is the record of Sprint 9 and Submission 9; the Week 10 report and the reflection carry the later state and cite it.
- **The 107 points are read as a 100-point scale.**
  The syllabus states the points and the pass threshold: ten group submissions at 7 points (70), 10 points attendance, 7 points individual reflection and peer evaluation, and 20 points final exam, for 107 points, with the pass at 75 points.
- **A deck draft is expected in Submission 9.**
  There is no draft: the one deck is Submission 10's; the Week 9 script and review do not show it, and a team that brings one declares the deviation and does not submit it in Submission 9.
- **The Week 10 report records its own `main` commit.**
  It cannot: the report records the `REL-10` tag and its commit, and the wrapper carries the final `main` permalink and snapshot; a report in the final commit cannot contain that commit's own hash.
- **The handoff claims to transfer the private data.**
  It records the retention: the team keeps the private repository and the recordings until the course is graded and deletes them after; the wrapper's `## For customer` carries the access and the deletion date, and the public report says the private values are there.
- **The MVP claim overstates what was verified.**
  The final report cites the focus metric, the `UT-01` results, and the customer's acceptance, and the verification's missed journey is a known issue or an open item; the report does not claim a journey it did not check.
