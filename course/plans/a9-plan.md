## Design Decisions

Week 9 and Week 10 are one final sprint, one Sprint Review, one handoff, one final release, one final report, and one submission.

- **The final sprint.**
  `SPRINT-09` opens in Week 9 and ends at its Sprint Review in Week 10; the team schedules the review in Week 10, and the review is before the handoff and the handoff before Submission 9.
  The sprint's `**Due:**` and its GitHub milestone carry the review date, not `2026-12-03`; Submission 9 is due `2026-12-03` and is the assignment's deadline, not the sprint's.
  The final sprint is planned like every other sprint: its goal replaces the provisional one, its `**Sprint Backlog:**` links the final milestone, its `**Planned Velocity:**` is the story points of the stories it schedules, and its `**Actual Velocity:**` is set at the close.
  The deck, the final report, the handoff preparation, the repository finalization, and the release are tasks with no story points; a product story carried from `SPRINT-08` or added in Week 9 counts.
  The final sprint's product backlog is refined first, and every open issue is either scheduled in it, closed as not planned with a reason, or recorded as a handoff open item; no issue is left for a sprint that does not exist.
  The recommended schedule is the Sprint Review on Tue 1 December, the handoff on Wed 2 December, and the freeze and submission on Thu 3 December; the team may choose earlier dates, provided the review is before the handoff and the handoff is before the submission.
- **The review and the handoff.**
  The Week 10 weekly customer meeting is the Sprint Review and the only Sprint Review.
  The handoff is a separate customer meeting after the sprint closes and before Submission 9; it is not a second review, and the review does not defer its own verdict to it.
  The review shows the final product, the 7-minute deck, the final report draft, the handoff package, and the customer's final story verdicts; it discusses the handoff and records what is ready and what is missing in the final report's `## Handoff preparation`; the team applies what it can before the sprint close, and anything that cannot close becomes a handoff open item.
  The handoff is a working meeting where the team tells the customer what is handed over: the `REL-09` release, the repository, the deployed product, the documentation, the dashboards and access, the credentials, the known issues, the open items, and the private data access and deletion date.
  The handoff has its own script and report, with a transcript when permitted; the report carries the handoff package and the customer's acceptance, and the acceptance is a `DEC-nnn` that the final report's `## Handoff` cites.
  The handoff is live; the existing asynchronous rule is a last resort with the written exchange as the transcript and a declared deviation, not an accepted alternative.
  Week 10's two meetings are `meeting-report.md` and `meeting-report-2.md`, in chronological order, as the meeting rule already says.
  The handoff is the course's own project transition: it uses the course's meeting, report, and release rules and does not import the SWP transition artifacts or vocabulary.
- **The final report.**
  `reports/week-09/README.md` is the weekly report and the index; `reports/week-09/final-report.md` is the final project report.
  The final report is the narrative a reader who does not know the project reads once: the engineering journey, the technical choices, the outcomes, the quality goals achieved, the team dynamics, the handoff preparation, the handoff, the lessons learned, and what the team would do differently.
  The outcomes cite the threshold of success and the testing plan; the technical choices cite the architecture, the ADRs, and the deployment; the usability and configuration work is cited through `docs/usability-tests/ut-01/results.md` and `docs/configuration-plan.md`, not repeated.
  The team dynamics section describes the team's way of working, its roles, its conflicts, and the retrospectives; it rates no individual, and the weekly report's contribution table is the evidence of who did what.
  The `## Handoff preparation` is written from the Sprint Review, and the `## Handoff` is written after the handoff meeting; both cite the meeting reports and the `DEC-nnn` and action points they produced.
  The lessons learned name the problems that taught them, and the "what we would do differently" names the changes the team would make in the next project; both cite the `SPRINT-04` to `SPRINT-09` retrospectives and the risk register rather than inventing a lesson.
  The final report is public and sanitized: no raw participant data, no private address, no credential, and no individual rating.
- **The deck.**
  The deck is a 7-minute product pitch, prepared in Week 9, submitted with Submission 9, rehearsed in Week 10, and presented at the Week 11 final exam.
  It follows the lecture's structure: the audience and the goal, the opportunity, the story and the live demo, what the evidence supports, and how the product continues.
  It cites the `UT-01` results, the configuration plan, and the final report rather than restating them; the live demo runs on the frozen deployed product, and the deck says what the demo shows.
  The deck is Moodle-only: it is not committed to the repository, the weekly report names it as submitted on Moodle, and the Moodle submission carries the PDF.
  The submitted deck is the basis; the team may refine it before the final exam, because the exam is the presentation and the code freeze keeps the demonstrated product stable.
- **The code freeze and the final release.**
  The product code is frozen at the sprint close, and the formal `2026-12-03` code freeze is the hard gate and the submission deadline; the report, the weekly report, the deck, the changelog's released section, and the `REL-09` block may still merge, but no product code changes after the sprint close.
  `REL-09` is cut at the sprint close on the final product commit, before the handoff, and its tag is protected; the handoff cites the tag, and the final report's `## Handoff` records it.
  The release body is the handoff and upgrade note for the customer: what the release is, how to run it, what is known, whether the upgrade requires anything beyond deploying, and the `[Full changelog]` line that closes it.
  The plan's `#### REL-09` block carries `**Released:**` and `**Checked QRs:**`; a sprint with no checked requirement says `None` and keeps the testing-plan sentence; a final release with no product change says so and requires no upgrade, but it is still the version the handoff cites.
  The changelog's released section, the `REL-09` block, and the report are merged after the tag, as the release rule already orders them; the final snapshot includes the report and the docs, while the tag stays on the final product commit.
- **The final repository state.**
  Before the handoff, every final pull request is merged; there is no open pull request, and the `SPRINT-09` milestone is closed after its last issue is closed or recorded.
  Every open issue is either closed as not planned with a reason or recorded as an open item in the final report's `## Handoff`; there is no later sprint to move it to, so the handoff is where it lands.
  The default branch is green; the documentation site, `docs/setup.md`, `docs/deployment.md`, the architecture, the requirements, the quality requirements and their tests, the results, the configuration plan, the features, the glossary, the risks, and the team's `AGENTS.md` describe the final state.
  The final production deployment is the `REL-09` commit, verified on the deployed product; the private usability-data repository and the recordings folder remain accessible to the instructors and are deleted after the course is graded.

## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **The handoff finds something that needs product code.**
  The team cannot fix it after the sprint close; it becomes an open item in the handoff, a risk, and a task for the next project, and the final report says so; the customer's acceptance may be conditional, and the `DEC-nnn` records the condition.
- **The customer rejects the handoff.**
  The handoff report records the rejection and the open items, the customer's decision is a `DEC-nnn`, and the final report's `## Handoff` says what was not accepted; no product code changes after the sprint close, and the submission still goes in.
- **The handoff is too late to write the final report's `## Handoff`.**
  The team holds the handoff at least one day before the submission; if it slips, the `## Handoff` is a short record of what was handed over with the open items named, and the deviations section declares the slip; the deadline is not moved.
- **The sprint does not end before the handoff.**
  The handoff is moved after the sprint; the team schedules the review earlier; the handoff still has to be before Submission 9, and a handoff after the deadline is a missing deliverable.
- **The review is on the sprint's last day.**
  The team applies the review's gaps in the same day before the sprint close, cuts `REL-09` on the final product commit, and holds the handoff the next day; the same-day rework is the release rule's "same-week rework" in its tightest form.
- **The deck is not ready for Submission 9.**
  The deck is a required deliverable and its Moodle upload is the submission; the team rehearses and submits it, and the final exam is the next day; a missing deck is a missing deliverable, not a deviation.
- **The final release has no product change.**
  `REL-09` is still the handoff version: its body says no product change and no upgrade is required, its tag is on the final product commit, and the `REL-09` block says `**Checked QRs:** None` and keeps the testing-plan sentence.
- **The final report names an address, a credential, or a person.**
  The public report is sanitized: the address and the credential stay in the wrapper, the person is named by GitHub username, and the handoff report and the final report remove the value; a leaked credential follows the incident response.
- **An issue cannot close before the final state.**
  It is closed as not planned with a reason and recorded as an open item in the final report's `## Handoff`, or it is recorded as an open item without a false close; the `SPRINT-09` milestone closes after it, and there is no later sprint.
- **The private data is deleted before grading.**
  It is not: the private repository and the recordings folder stay accessible to the team and the instructors until the course is graded, and the handoff records the deletion date; the final report records the retention.
- **The final exam uses a newer deck than the submitted one.**
  The submitted deck is the basis, the final exam may use the refined one, and the code freeze keeps the demonstrated product stable; the final report does not link the deck and the Moodle submission carries the submitted PDF.
- **The final report's contribution and team dynamics overlap.**
  The weekly report's contribution table is the evidence of who did what; the final report's team dynamics section describes the team's way of working and the retrospectives and rates no individual, so the two do not duplicate the same judgment.
