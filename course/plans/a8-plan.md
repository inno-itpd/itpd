## Design Decisions

Week 8 runs the usability test and reviews the configuration; the results claim what the sessions found, the fixes are planned and triaged, and the configuration review is full-depth with the evaluation as the deliverable; no separate plan and no implementation are required.

- **The week.**
  Week 8 has two streams: the run week of `UT-01`, and the configuration review.
  The run week is the design Assignment 7's plan already wrote: the at-least-three counted sessions, the sanitized session notes, `results.md`, the findings, the triage, the `QR/QRT` verdict, the threshold outcome, `REL-08`, the Sprint Review, the retrospective, and the AI usage report.
  The configuration review is the new stream: the map and the evaluation; the evaluation carries the Keep, Cut, And Simplify recommendation.
  Week 9 prepares the deck and finalizes the repository; the evaluation's recommendations are the handoff, and no implementation is required.
  The split is what lets the week run the test in the days the results need and still review the configuration without a second sprint.
- **The review's scope.**
  The review judges the whole repository configuration: the delivery configuration and the context an agent reads.
  The five groups are source control and release, checks, environments and secrets, dependencies and builds, and agent context and artifacts.
  The criteria are the course's own configuration rules: the branch protection, the link check, the pinned actions, CI, the environments, the secrets, the contributing guide, the `AGENTS.md`, the identifiers, the links, and the weekly report.
  The course materials are an upstream item the team installs and wires in, and it judges them but does not edit them; the review's improvement is in the team's own repository, such as a shorter `AGENTS.md`, a repository map that points at the skill's router, or one workflow instead of four.
  A review that judges only the product code would miss the configuration the agent actually reads; a review that judges only the agent context would miss the checks and the delivery that make the product trustworthy.
- **The map.**
  `docs/configuration-management.md` is maintained documentation, and it is the configuration's inventory: what the items are, what each is for, which properties matter, what each reads and what reads it, and where it lives.
  It carries at least 12 `CFG-nn` items in the five groups, one section per item at `### CFG-01`, headed by the identifier alone, with `**Status:**` `Active`, `Planned`, or `Dropped`, `**Purpose:**`, `**Properties:**`, `**Connections:**`, and `**Where:**`, a `**Dropped:**` field when an item is removed, and a `**Planned:**` field with the sprint or `Later` and the reason when the item is planned.
  It carries a connections table, `| From | To | Kind | What breaks |`, where the kind is `reads`, `enforces`, `produces`, `triggers`, or `cites`, and it says what breaks when the connection is lost.
  It records the baseline it describes and the date it was last reviewed, and it is updated in the same pull request when the change alters what it records; a settings change that leaves the map accurate needs no update, and a settings change that alters the map is recorded in the pull request that closes the task, because a map that describes last month's repository is worse than none: it is believed.
  The map is the maintained artifact a reader or an agent opens to find the configuration; the evaluation is the judgement of it at one point, and it is not maintained.
- **The evaluation.**
  `reports/week-08/cm-evaluation.md` is the week's judgement of the map.
  It names its baseline, the tag or commit it reviewed and the date, so a later week can tell what state the verdicts describe.
  It carries the method, the verdict for each item, the cost of each item, the strengths to keep, the problems, at least two traced paths, the not-planned list, and the conclusion with the Keep, Cut, And Simplify recommendation.
  The verdict is `Adequate`, `Weak`, `Missing`, or `Too heavy`: adequate is an item that does its job at a cost the team can carry, weak is an item whose property fails or whose connection is missing in a way that matters, missing is a configuration the repository needs and does not have, and too heavy is an item that works but costs more than it returns for the team.
  The cost is stated, not implied: the maintenance time, the review surface, the tokens an agent spends when it reads the item, and what fails when the item drifts.
  A problem that endangers a goal, a quality requirement, or a risk cites it, so a `Weak` item tied to `QR-02` is different from a `Weak` item that is housekeeping; an item with no tie is recorded as upkeep with its reason, and the `upkeep` classification is defined in the configuration-management requirements rather than borrowed from [The Issue Form](../../requirements/task-issues-requirements.md#the-issue-form).
  The evaluation is a judgement, so an `Adequate` verdict needs its evidence as much as a failing one does.
- **The cost and the too-heavy test.**
  The `Too heavy` verdict is the week's distinctive judgement: an item whose cost exceeds the value it returns.
  The cost model is the maintenance time, the number of files or steps a change touches, the tokens an agent spends to read it, and the failure cost when it drifts.
  The test is the team's size and the remaining course: a team of the course's size with two sprints left cannot carry a second telemetry backend, a fourth dashboard, a custom release script, or an `AGENTS.md` longer than the repository it describes.
  The evaluation's right-sizing is the removal, merge, or simplification the verdict implies, and the evaluation classifies at least one item as `Too heavy` or explains with its cost estimates why the configuration is right-sized.
  The distinction matters because a review that only reports missing items pushes a team to add configuration; the course wants the team to be able to take configuration away.
- **The recommendation.**
  `reports/week-08/cm-evaluation.md` is the week's judgement, and its `## Keep, Cut, And Simplify` section is the recommendation.
  It says what the team would keep as it is, what it would remove or merge, and what it would simplify, each with the `CFG-nn` it keeps, cuts, or simplifies, or the gap it fills, the change, the reason, and the goal, quality requirement, or risk it serves or its upkeep.
  It carries a not-planned list: the changes the team considered and dropped, with the reason, so the recommendation reads as a design rather than a wish list.
  No separate plan is required: the recommendation is the evaluation's conclusion, not a second document, and the team does not schedule the changes as a roadmap.
- **No separate plan and no implementation.**
  The maintainer's decision is that the week evaluates; it does not require the changes and it does not require a separate plan.
  The syllabus's deliverable 3 is an implementation, and the plan changes it to an optional implementation with the evaluation required, because a team that has time can enact a change and a team that does not is not penalised for the six-day week.
  An optional change is the same work under the existing rules: a task issue, a pull request, the map updated in the pull request that records the change, the green checks, and the release when the change is product-visible.
  The evaluation is what keeps the week's analysis honest: it is judged on its own evidence, not on how much the team changed.
- **The usability results.**
  The results are the run week's evidence: at least three counted sessions, the sanitized notes, the per-participant table, the main task's aggregate completion, the median time, the median SEQ for each task, the SUS mean and median with the `n=<count>` caveat, the instrumentation comparison, the thematic analysis, the limitations, and the conclusion.
  The findings are `### Finding 1` sections with their severity, the tasks and participant codes, the evidence, the recommendation, and the task issue; the triage records what the team does with each finding.
  A significant finding becomes a task issue scheduled in `SPRINT-08`; a fix that lands is in `REL-08`; a fix that does not land moves to `SPRINT-09` and the report says so; no fix is forced when the test finds nothing significant.
  The `QR/QRT` verdict is in the testing plan, not in `results.md`: `Passes` or `Fails`, `Checked: SPRINT-08`, and the results as its evidence.
  The threshold's usability goal is settled when the team has one; a team without one is not penalised, because the quality requirement and its test are the required check.
- **The public review.**
  The map and the evaluation are public and sanitized.
  They name no secret value, no private deployment or dashboard address, and no exploitable weakness; a detail that names one goes in the Moodle wrapper, and the public file gives a placeholder and says why.
  A leaked credential follows the existing incident response: revoke or rotate, tell the instructor, clean the history, and write the account privately.
  The private details of the week's run are the private usability-data repository and the recordings folder, which the wrapper links for the instructors; the configuration review adds the wrapper's private appendix when it needs one.
- **The identifiers.**
  `CFG-nn` is the Week 8 family: two digits, repository-wide, introduced by the requirement that first uses it.
  The section heading is the identifier alone, `### CFG-01`, the first line names the item, and the fields follow as bullets, per [Identifier Rules](../../requirements/general-requirements.md#identifier-rules).
  The evaluation cites the `CFG-nn`, and a reader finds the item from the map's heading anchor.
  A dropped item keeps its heading and fields, with `**Status:**` `Dropped` and a `**Dropped:**` date and reason, and a planned item keeps its heading and fields, with `**Status:**` `Planned` and a `**Planned:**` sprint or `Later` and reason, so the map's history is readable and no identifier is reused.
- **The requirements owner and the guide.**
  `requirements/configuration-management-requirements.md` owns the map, the items, the connections, the evaluation and its recommendation, the public review, and the example, and it links the course's configuration rules rather than restating them.
  `guides/configuration-management.md` shows the method: the baseline, the inventory, the connections, the delivery and knowledge paths, the judgement, the cost, the recommendation, the sanitized publication, and the handoff.
  The guide gives the property bank per group, so a team does not invent the properties it judges, and it gives the delivery and knowledge path patterns and the Keep, Cut, And Simplify patterns, so a recommendation is provable.
  The method is a guide rather than a second requirements file because it explains how to judge, and the rules say what the artifact must contain.
- **The syllabus.**
  Week 8's class line, its focus row, its objectives, its deliverables, and its `Detailed requirements` line follow the deck and the assignment.

## Edge Cases And Failure Modes

- **The team uses no coding agent.**
  The agent-context group is still reviewed: the map, the docs, the identifiers, the links, the `AGENTS.md`, and the skill's installation are configuration, and a team that does not install the skill records the decision and judges the router it does use; the course does not require an agent.
- **The course materials are read-only.**
  The review judges them as an upstream item and judges the team's wiring of them; an improvement is a shorter `AGENTS.md`, a repository map that points at the router, or a smaller set of instructions, never an edit to the submodule.
- **The review exposes a secret or an address.**
  The public file carries a placeholder and the reason, the private detail goes in the Moodle appendix, and a leaked credential is revoked or rotated and reported per the incident response.
- **A check is red or slow.**
  The verdict is `Weak`, the evaluation's recommendation is the fix, and the release waits for a green `main`; a check that is not applicable is recorded with its reason, per the existing CI rule.
- **A check's exclusion is not justified.**
  The exclusion is a configuration item with its reason in the link check or the Markdown configuration; an unjustified exclusion is a `Weak` verdict and a recommendation.
- **The map grows longer than the repository.**
  The map is a `Too heavy` candidate itself: the evaluation recommends trimming it to the items an agent or a reader needs, and the guide's common mistakes name it.
- **The review would change too much.**
  The evaluation's recommendation is scoped, the not-planned list records what it does not do, and no change is forced.
- **An optional change is enacted.**
  It is a task issue, a pull request, the map update in that pull request, the green checks, and the release when it is product-visible; it is extra evidence, not a requirement.
- **A session does not count.**
  The A7 rule holds: the raw text and the recording are removed, the code is retired, a replacement is recruited under the next code, and the report says how many counted sessions there were.
- **Fewer than three sessions run.**
  The `QR/QRT` row stays `Not verified` or moves to `SPRINT-09` with a reason, the results claim no result, and the configuration review still runs; the report declares the shortfall.
- **The results are late and the configuration review is on time.**
  The report carries the configuration section and the results section with the open session window declared, and the Week 9 plan carries the results when they land.
- **The recommendation changes after the results.**
  The review records the decision as a `DEC-nnn`, the evaluation's recommendation changes, and the report says what changed.
- **The team cannot judge a configuration item.**
  The evaluation records the item with the reason it could not be judged and the risk it carries, rather than a verdict it cannot support.
- **The Week 9 handoff slips.**
  The map is the maintained artifact, the evaluation is the Week 8 judgement, the results are the deck's evidence, the private data stays until grading, and the Week 9 plan owns the deck and the final state.
